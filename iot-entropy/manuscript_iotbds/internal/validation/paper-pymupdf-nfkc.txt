 Does Entropy Help Detect IoT Faults? A Comparative
             Study with Generative AI

                        Anonymous Author(s)


       Abstract. A faulty sensor may begin copying its neighbors while producing
       apparently plausible readings. Detecting such changes requires examining both
        relationships between sensors and patterns within individual streams. We investigate
        spatial and temporal entropy as complementary measurements for IoT fault
        detection through a GPU implementation that compares conventional statistics with
       entropy features under historical resampling and generative diffusion references.
       Experiments cover 360 fault realizations across 20 simulations and recording
        blocks, using one synthetic dataset and two real datasets with injected faults.
       Temporal entropy and autocorrelation capture changes the spatial covariance can
        ignore. On Intel data, temporal entropy with resampling achieves recall 0.389
       versus 0.000 for conventional temporal statistics, with respective background
       alarm rates of 0.103 and 0.000. Missing data analysis helps explain observed
       performance differences. Adding entropy to conventional features yields limited
       incremental benefit, and neither entropy nor the evaluated diffusion reference
        provides a consistent overall advantage. The findings characterize complementary
      measurement capabilities and provide practical guidance to evaluating entropy
       and generative models in sensor networks, with the benefits depending on fault
       mechanism, measurement availability and calibration.

      Keywords: IoT · spatial entropy · permutation entropy · sample entropy · diffusion
        forecasting · fault localization

1  Introduction

Sensors in digital twins and data spaces need evidence about changes in both collective
organization and individual dynamics. Copying streams can reduce independent spatial
variation, replay can alter the temporal organization without creating a large marginal
outlier. A legitimate operating transition can also change both. An alert should therefore
expose measurements and their provenance, rather than assert an unverified physical
cause.
   This study compares information captured by spatial and temporal entropy with
conventional measurements, separating the features from its normal reference model.
There is a motivating connection to prior work, that entropy trajectories describe evolving
homogeneous clustering under different network topologies [9] and collective flocking
organization [10]. We transfer attention to evolving organization and interaction structure.
The segregation study uses degree/homophily distributions, the flocking work develops
a thermodynamic description of its simulation. Here spatial entropy uses normalized
correlation eigenvalues, permutation entropy uses ordinal patterns, and sample entropy
uses matching templates. These definitions differ. We infer neither thermodynamic
quantities nor a second law for sensor recordings.
   The earlier version of this project evaluated spatial correlation spectrum entropy
and established no consistent improvement over its synchronization comparators. That
finding motivates, but does not predetermine, this extension. A changing spatial entropy
trace is not itself a within sensor temporal entropy estimator. Nor does an unsuccessful
reference model establish a limitation of all entropy features. The original artifacts
remain identifiable, new comparisons, support corrections and diagnostics have separate
manifests.
  We address six practical and theoretical questions. RQ1: does spatial entropy improve
detection or localization over non-entropic spatial statistics? RQ2: do temporal entropies
reveal changes missed by spatial covariance summaries? RQ3: do they improve over
conventional temporal statistics? RQ4: does combining spatial and temporal evidence
improve detection or localization? RQ5: does a generative reference improve over joint
block bootstrap? RQ6: how do support, missingness, window length, calibration and
mechanism affect these answers?
   Our contribution is a reproducible comparison, elementary theoretical characterization
of the information retained by its measurements, and an inspectable monitoring artifact.
We introduce neither an entropy definition nor a general purpose diffusion architecture.
Conventional features augmented with entropy are compared directly with conventional
features alone; entropy is not required to win or lose.

3.3  Conventional temporal measurements and feature families
Temporal baselines measure persistence through autocorrelation (ACF) at lags 1, 2 and
4 and changes in this vector. Variability is measured by first difference variance and
local sample variance, drift by a least squares trend, and mean departures by predictive
deviation and a two-sided CUSUM. Lag pairs preserve actual time positions. Each
statistic requires at least max(12, ⌈푊/2⌉) observations or valid pairs. CUSUM resets ateach window, uses allowance .5 in training-scaled units, pauses on missing rows, and
reports its final state. Reference scoring expresses measurements as departures from
expected behavior.
    Additional detectors use raw predictive residuals and the retained GDN adaptation.
Raw residuals average absolute pointwise deviations divided by (generated MAD plus .05),
retaining the original spatial quality restriction. GDN uses 32-dimensional embeddings,
learned top-15 neighborhoods, graph attention and a shared two-layer head for multiple
channels without batch normalization. Errors standardized on development data are
averaged within four trailing one step predictions before taking the group maximum.
GDN receives causal observed target history, giving it more recent information than the
long-lead references. Its group support and participation mapping are adaptations, so
published GDN benchmark accuracy is not claimed. Feature families comprise spatial
entropy (S), permutation entropy (PE), sample entropy (SE), both temporal entropies
(T), and all entropies (ST). Conventional families are spatial (SB), temporal (TB), and
combined (B). BST adds all entropies to B. Families use the maximum component score.
Unsupported components are skipped, with abstention if none qualify. Level/change
pairs require both endpoints. Comparing B with BST tests whether entropy improves
conventional detectors.

3.6  Localization and availability

Group participation weights each group’s score by inverse size and normalizes over
eligible incident groups. Direct temporal ranking uses sensor scores, the combined rule
averages their normalized rank percentiles. S/SB use participation, T/PE/SE/TB direct
ranking, and ST/B/BST the combined rule. All three alternatives are also reported. The
budget is selected on development data, never from test truth. Candidate group best
overlap IoU is an oracle diagnostic, not a deployable method. Participation is not causal
attribution.
    Operational analysis includes every scheduled unit and injected event, includ-
ing abstentions and misses. A separately calibrated common support analysis uses
group/window/channel units valid for S, PE, SE, SB and TB within each reference.
Reference comparisons also report their differing model support counts, common feature
support is not claimed to eliminate every reference support difference. Conditional
localization is reported only alongside unconditional scores, where misses contribute
zero. Quality flags, signed agreement and onset alignment are descriptive and do not
create additional uncalibrated detections.

6  Results

6.1  Fault detection, localization and added value

Table 1 and Fig. 3 distinguish detecting faults, measured by recall, from identifying
affected sensors, measured by IoU. Background rates describe the alarm burden on
data without injected faults. For spatial measurements (RQ1), detection gains often
accompany more background alarms. Synthetic diffusion S minus SB gives recall
−0.099 [−0.174, −0.034] and IoU −0.010 [−0.017, −0.003]. Intel diffusion S detectsfewer faults than SB (0.213 versus 0.481), with fewer background alarms (0.218 versus
0.372). PEMS bootstrap S detects more faults (0.528 versus 0.005), but its background
rate is 0.301 versus 0.000. Capping background rates retrospectively at .10 reverses this
ordering, with recalls 0.069 versus 0.264 and rates 0.026 versus 0.064. This illustrates
threshold sensitivity, without validating future alarm control.
   Temporal entropy (RQ3) helps in some settings. Synthetic bootstrap T/TB recall
is 0.401/0.532, a difference of −0.131 [−0.313, +0.054], with IoU 0.093/0.239. Intel
bootstrap T exceeds TB by +0.389 [+0.264, +0.523] recall, at background rates 0.103versus 0.000. Sample entropy has higher Intel recall and sensor overlap than permutation
entropy, with recall 0.380 versus 0.310 and IoU 0.073 versus 0.022. Diffusion reduces
Intel T recall to 0.060 while increasing background alarms to 0.288. PEMS temporal
comparisons remain less specific.
   Combining features (RQ4) does not automatically preserve their individual strengths.
Synthetic bootstrap ST improves recall over S by +0.123 [+0.028, +0.230], but over T by
only +0.008 [−0.085, +0.127]. Their localization rules differ. Additional comparisonshold these rules fixed. Adding entropy to conventional B increases synthetic bootstrap
recall by +0.003 [+0.000, +0.008] and IoU by +0.008 [+0.000, +0.017], using identicallocalization rules. Recall remains unchanged in the other five dataset/reference combina-
tions. Conventional components usually dominate the maximum score, and each family
has its own calibrated threshold. For Intel bootstrap, T detects faults while B and BST
detect none at the selected threshold. This does not establish feature equivalence.
    Additional detectors offer different tradeoffs. GDN synthetic/Intel/PEMS recall is
0.514/0.139/0.157, with background rates 0.135/0.436/0.038. PEMS bootstrap residuals
achieve recall 0.264 at background rate 0.077. Their information access and support differ.
Localization remains difficult. For detected faults, bootstrap T IoU is 0.231/0.195/0.038
across the three datasets, falling to 0.093/0.076/0.015 when missed faults count as zero.

6.2  Complementary responses to different fault mechanisms
RQ2 shows how spatial and temporal measurements complement each other. In 64
aligned simulations, rearranging time points identically across sensors preserves their
correlation matrix to within 1.3 × 10−15 (rounded upward). Mean PE rises from 0.964to 0.989 and SE from 0.586 to 1.115, while ACF1 falls from 0.845 to -0.016. Spatial
entropy remains unchanged. Copying lowers spatial entropy from 0.877 to 0.134, while
PE changes from 0.964 to 0.965. Additional diagnostics examine offsets, gains, sign
changes, quantization, replay, periodic interference, irregularity, legitimate transitions
and dropout. These diagnostics isolate responses separately from the primary benchmark.
   The synthetic benchmark demonstrates practical strengths. Bootstrap temporal entropy
detects 74.1% of flatline events versus 24.1% for conventional temporal measurements.
For independent noise, conventional recall is 1.000 versus 0.463 for temporal entropy.
These responses occur at unequal achieved background alarm rates. Windows spanning
a flatline onset can retain measurable entropy, while wholly constant windows trigger
abstention. Paired results distinguish mechanism, severity, duration, measured direction
and affected data availability. Direction denotes the observed lagged difference. The
first decision after onset occurs 11 samples later, highlighting how window length and
decision frequency influence detection of short events.

7  Dashboard and GPU Computation

The dashboard links alerts to sensor behavior. Operators can inspect contributing sensors,
coordinated changes and departures from generated references. Fixed sensor positions
support visual comparison, with separate overlays for physical connections and correlation
changes.
    Selecting a group reveals individual entropy traces, conventional measurements and
predictive bands. The display separates actual increases or decreases from departures
relative to expected behavior. Template and pair counts indicate whether enough obser-
vations support an estimate. Shared channels, change direction and onset timing help
users investigate an alert without assigning a physical cause. Forecast and observation
times, model version and the scan calibrated p-value provide context. Three prespecified
copying examples demonstrate the workflow without selecting for success.
  CUDA batches correlation matrices, eigenvalues, ordinal patterns, conventional
statistics and joint trajectory generation. Sample entropy runs in bounded batches to
limit temporary memory. Statistical calculations use float32 and are checked against
CPU float64, separately from neural mixed precision. Stored predictions, calibration
results, missingness information and reference trajectories support reconstruction of the
displayed evidence.
   On an RTX A6000, median time from reference generation through sensor localization
is 0.180, 0.174 and 0.258 seconds for bootstrap on synthetic64, Intel and PEMS,
respectively. Diffusion takes 0.370, 0.338 and 1.164 seconds. Peak allocated memory is
at most 1.43 GiB. These timings include missingness handling, both windows, feature
extraction, scoring and localization. They exclude GDN, data acquisition, loading, disk
output, rank lookup and dashboard rendering.
   For PEMS, the temporal calculation takes 0.724 seconds on CPU float64 and 0.035
seconds on GPU float32, excluding transfers. The comparison combines hardware and
precision differences, while maximum numerical disagreement remains below 2.0×10−7.Timed GPU stages totaled 4.271 hours across experiments, validation and interrupted
attempts. The core computation is shorter than the real datasets’ sampling intervals.
Overall response time also includes data handling, window accumulation and decision
scheduling.

8  Conclusion

This study provides an integrated approach to evaluating the entropy for IoT fault
detections. The contribution is the joint assessment of measurement information, data
availability, generative reference quality and alarm calibration, supported by GPU
computation and an inspectable dashboard. Spatial and temporal measurements capture
different changes, supporting feature selection according to sensor behavior and fault
mechanisms.
