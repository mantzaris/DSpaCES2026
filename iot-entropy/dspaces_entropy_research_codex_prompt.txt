GROUPS AND ENTROPY DEFINITION
Let G=(V,E) be the fixed physical/proximity graph. Predefine overlapping local groups g from graph neighborhoods and documented metadata. Initial sizes may be 6, 12, and 24 nodes where data coverage permits. Deduplicate groups and freeze their definitions before testing. Include several window sizes, initially W in {24,48,96}, but use only group/window pairs with adequate sample support. Do not assume normalized entropy has identical finite-sample behavior across group sizes or window lengths.

For a group of m sensors and one measurement channel in a window ending at t, let Z contain the n_valid common rows after centering and scaling each sensor column to unit sample variance. Define:
R_(g,t) = Z^T Z / (n_valid - 1).
Rtilde_(g,t) = (1 - lambda) R_(g,t) + lambda I_m.
p_k = eigenvalue_k(Rtilde_(g,t)) / trace(Rtilde_(g,t)).
H_(g,t) = -sum_k p_k log(p_k) / log(m).

Use 0 log 0 = 0. Choose lambda on development data, keep its handling identical for observations and generated samples, and document that positive shrinkage raises the minimum attainable entropy. Calculate eigenvalues with a symmetric eigensolver. Symmetrize numerical roundoff, reject materially non-PSD matrices, and only clip eigenvalues within a documented floating-point tolerance. Compare GPU calculations against float64 CPU reference cases.

H is a local correlation-spectrum entropy. It reflects the effective dimensionality of co-fluctuation within spatially selected groups. The spatial content comes from graph-based grouping and the spatial scan; H alone ignores sensor ordering and geometric arrangement inside a fixed group. It is not the Shannon entropy of sensor coordinates or of a graph degree histogram. Low H can reflect strong positive or negative dependence; preserve the signs of pairwise correlations in explanations.

Define entropy change DeltaH_(g,t) = H_(g,t) - H_(g,t-d) for fixed lag d. Initial d can be W/4, rounded and fixed by configuration. Use both H and DeltaH. Compute each channel separately; aggregate standardized anomaly scores later. A single global entropy trace is an ablation.

NEW SCORE TO IMPLEMENT AND STUDY
For each group/channel/window, let u_(g,t) = [H_(g,t), DeltaH_(g,t)] and let u_(g,t)^(b) be computed from generated sample b. For component j define:
center_(g,t,j) = median_b u_(g,t,j)^(b).
scale_(g,t,j) = 1.4826 median_b |u_(g,t,j)^(b) - center_(g,t,j)| + epsilon_j.
A_(g,t)^H = max_j |u_(g,t,j) - center_(g,t,j)| / scale_(g,t,j).

THEORY TO DERIVE, VERIFY, AND IMPLEMENT
Result 1: For a PSD correlation matrix, show 0 <= H <= 1; at lambda=0, H=1 for I and H=0 for a rank-one correlation matrix. State the changed lower limit under positive shrinkage. Discuss finite samples, autocorrelation, and the rank ceiling when n_valid is too small.

Result 2: For R(rho)=(1-rho)I + rho 11^T with 0 <= rho < 1 and lambda=0, derive:
p_1=[1+(m-1)rho]/m; p_2...p_m=(1-rho)/m.
dH/drho = (m-1)/(m log m) * log[(1-rho)/(1+(m-1)rho)] <= 0.
For fixed positive shrinkage replace rho by (1-lambda)rho and apply the chain rule. Interpret this as a result for this family, not a universal monotonic law for all synchronizations or faults.

Result 3: Demonstrate a limit of average-correlation traces. For m=4, compare equicorrelation rho=0.2 (spectrum 1.6,0.8,0.8,0.8) with two separate correlated pairs rho=0.6 (spectrum 1.6,1.6,0.4,0.4). Both have mean pairwise correlation 0.2 and leading concentration 0.4. At lambda=0 their normalized entropies are approximately 0.960964 and 0.860964. Prove validity and reproduce numerically. This shows different organization at the same simple summaries, not that entropy beats full covariance information.

Result 4: State and prove the calibration rank property only under its actual assumptions, and evaluate its degradation for dependent sensor streams. This is an application of established conformal theory, not a new distribution-free time-series theorem.

Include limitations as explicit counterexamples: R and SRS for diagonal sign matrix S have equal spectra; fixed-group node permutations do not change H; offsets and positive rescaling can preserve correlations; nonzero-variance coherent faults and legitimate common drivers can share H; flatlined channels make Pearson correlations undefined.
