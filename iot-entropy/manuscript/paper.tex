\section{Introduction}
Sensor-fed digital twins depend on measurements whose collective behavior can
change before a single extreme value becomes conspicuous. A copied stream may
make several devices unusually alike; independent interference may break a
previously shared pattern. A legitimate operating transition can also change
coordination. A monitoring layer for data spaces should identify the affected
measurements, retain their provenance and expose this ambiguity rather than
equate a change with a verified physical failure.

We investigate an informational description of this behavior: the entropy of
the correlation spectrum within spatially selected sensor groups. A spectrum
concentrated in a few modes has low entropy; broadly distributed modes have
high entropy. Monitoring both directions can reveal different reorganizations.
However, direction alone is insufficient: expected seasonality, window length,
missing observations and changes in normal collective activity also affect the
trace. The question is whether comparing local entropy trajectories with a
conditional joint normal reference improves detection and localization at a
matched calibration budget, relative to non-entropic synchronization traces.

The motivating connection to Mantzaris's research is specific. Entropy
trajectories have been used to describe evolving segregation under different
network topologies~\cite{mantzaris2024} and collective flocking
organization~\cite{mantzaris2025}. We transfer the use of evolving entropy as a
description of collective organization, and the attention to the network that
defines interactions. The former study uses degree and homophily distributions;
the latter constructs thermodynamic quantities from a flocking model. Here the
probability vector consists of normalized eigenvalues of a \emph{sensor
correlation matrix}. This is not a degree-distribution entropy, a thermodynamic
entropy or a causal explanation. We establish no temperature, heat flow or
second-law claim.

Our contribution is a reproducible formulation and empirical test of local
level-and-change scores, historically issued generative reference blocks,
explicit quality abstention, scan calibration and inspectable localization.
The study separates feature choice from reference-model choice in a factorial
comparison, includes a full-correlation detector, and preserves negative
findings. The output is an attention signal with observed evidence; injected
faults, legitimate controls and unknown native causes remain distinct.

\section{Related Work}
Spectral entropy is established. Effective rank uses the exponential entropy of
normalized singular values~\cite{effective_rank}; for a PSD correlation matrix,
singular values equal eigenvalues. Graph-entropy monitoring also predates this
study: FINGER approximates von Neumann entropy of graph Laplacians and applies
graph distances to anomaly detection~\cite{finger}. Our correlation spectrum
differs from a Laplacian spectrum, and its spatial meaning comes from group
selection, not an ordering of sensors inside the matrix.

Diffusion forecasting and imputation are likewise prior tools. TimeGrad models
multivariate predictive distributions~\cite{timegrad}, CSDI conditions joint
diffusion on masked observations~\cite{csdi}, and DiffSTG incorporates
spatiotemporal graph structure~\cite{diffstg}. DiffAD~\cite{diffad},
ImDiffusion~\cite{imdiffusion} and ICDiffAD~\cite{icdiffad} demonstrate that
diffusion-based time-series anomaly detection is not new. Our information
boundary is a forecasting boundary: the main generator receives no observed
value from its target block. We ask about the resulting distributions of
collective measurements, rather than claiming a new denoising architecture.

GDN learns a sensor graph and forecasts values to detect departures from
inter-sensor relationships~\cite{gdn}. It motivates our strong graph baseline.
Conformal work with dependent observations explicitly addresses assumptions
needed beyond exchangeability~\cite{conformal}. Our simpler scan-max rank
calibration is an application of established rank reasoning, with empirical
time-series diagnostics, not a new distribution-free stream theorem.

\section{Local Measurements and Normal References}
\subsection{Spatial groups and data quality}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a fixed supplied physical or proximity graph. A predeclared
family $\calG$ contains deduplicated neighborhoods of sizes 6, 12 and 24 around
24 fixed index-spaced centers. Shortest graph distance selects membership;
geographic distance breaks ties. Windows $W\in\{24,48,96\}$ and lags $d=W/4$
are scanned only when $W\geq2|g|$. Channels are processed separately: temperature
and humidity never enter the same correlation matrix.

For one group/channel window, retain rows observed at every sensor. With $m$
columns and $n$ retained rows, require $n\geq\max(2m,\lceil.8W\rceil)$.
Otherwise abstain. Variance below $10^{-8}$ in training-scaled units gives a
separate flatline flag. It does not receive an entropy of zero. Neural inputs
use masked zero after training-only scaling, while feature calculation retains
the original missingness. Generated samples receive the observed window mask
\emph{after} generation to compare equal measurement support.

\subsection{Entropy and synchronization}
Center and standardize each retained column by its sample standard deviation,
forming $Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$. Define
\begin{align}
 \R_{g,t}&=Z^\top Z/(n-1),\qquad
 \widetilde\R_{g,t}=(1-\lambda)\R_{g,t}+\lambda I,\label{eq:correlation}\\
 p_k&=\frac{\lambda_k(\widetilde\R_{g,t})}{\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde\R_{g,t})},\qquad
 H_{g,t}=-\frac{\sum_k p_k\log p_k}{\log m},\label{eq:entropy}\\
 \Delta H_{g,t}&=H_{g,t}-H_{g,t-d}.\label{eq:change}
\end{align}
We use $0\log0=0$, $\lambda=.05$, a symmetric eigensolver and a roundoff
tolerance of $64m$ machine epsilons. Materially negative eigenvalues are an
error. The Gram product disables reduced-mantissa TF32; the network can use
mixed precision. CPU float64 cases check CUDA float32 measurements.

The non-entropic features are mean signed correlation $C$, mean absolute
correlation $C_{\rm abs}$, pairwise squared disagreement $D$, and leading
concentration $P=\lambda_{\max}(\R)/m$, each with a lagged change. With our
within-window sample normalization,
\begin{equation}
 D=\frac{1}{n{m\choose2}}\sum_{s,i<j}(z_{si}-z_{sj})^2
   =2\frac{n-1}{n}(1-C).\label{eq:redundancy}
\end{equation}
Thus $D$ is redundant with $C$ at fixed $n$. Low $H$ can reflect positive
\emph{or negative} dependence; displayed pairwise signs are retained.

\subsection{Joint conditional diffusion}
For the score at $t$, the generated block spans $t-119$ through $t$; it contains
both windows for the largest $W+d=120$. The 48-row conditioning history ends
at $t-120$, strictly before the target. Smaller windows use suffixes of the
same block, making their longer forecast lead explicit. Context includes
measurements, masks, fixed coordinates/graph and known calendar harmonics.

Our compact independent implementation follows the graph-temporal conditional
denoising ideas of DiffSTG/CSDI. A history encoder, coordinate/calendar
embeddings, width-24 gated temporal convolutions and three graph aggregation
blocks predict joint noise across all sensors. It is not a reproduction of
DiffSTG's UGnet. For noise step $s$,
\begin{align}
 Y_s&=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_s}Y+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_s}\epsilon,\\
 \mathcal L&=\E\!\left[\frac{\|M_{\rm loss}\odot
  (\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(Y_s,s,C,G))\|_F^2}{\sum M_{\rm loss}}\right].\label{eq:diffusion}
\end{align}
Only valid training targets enter the loss. The cosine schedule~\cite{cosine}
uses 100 steps; compatible DDIM~\cite{ddim} uses 20 inference steps, independent
initial noises and $\eta=0$. Denoised states are clipped to $[-8,8]$ in
training-scaled units. Three seeds, Adam at $10^{-3}$, batch 8 and at most
2400 updates are used; development checks every 200 updates select checkpoints,
with patience four. The main sample count is 64, chunked by 16.

The bootstrap reference samples intact joint training blocks from 64 candidate
donors selected by historical context and calendar similarity. Selection sees
no target values. This preserves empirical temporal/cross-sensor organization
and provides the same feature-by-reference factorial comparison. Neither model
identifies causal counterfactuals of real failures.

\subsection{Trajectory scores, calibration and localization}
For $u=(H,\Delta H)$, generated samples provide componentwise median $c_j$ and
scale $s_j=1.4826\operatorname{median}_b|u_j^{(b)}-c_j|+\epsilon_j$. The floor is
5\% of the development IQR, with numerical minimum $10^{-4}$, then frozen.
The proposed bidirectional trajectory score is
\begin{equation}
 A^H_{g,t}=\max_{j\in\{1,2\}}\frac{|u_{g,t,j}-c_{g,t,j}|}{s_{g,t,j}}.\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
MAD scaling implies no Gaussian tail model. Synchronization and combined
scores use the same operation on their declared feature components. At least
80\% of reference samples must support a feature. Independent comparisons
include Frobenius distance to the sample-mean full correlation matrix, raw-value
predictive residuals, a restarted finite-memory two-sided CUSUM and a labeled
GDN reimplementation. GDN uses causal observed target history for one-step
prediction, an advantage over the longer reference forecast; its learned
top-$k$ graph and graph attention are retained, while its head, normalization
and group mapping are explicit adaptations. Quality hybrids are reported
separately from entropy-only detection.

The scan maximum $M_t=\max_{g\in\calG} A_{g,t}$ includes every declared
group, channel and eligible window. All-abstained units have $M_t=-\infty$.
Disjoint context-plus-target calibration units, separated by 168 observations,
produce $M_1,\ldots,M_n$. We use
\begin{equation}
 p_t=\frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^n\mathbb{1}\{M_b\geq M_t\}}{n+1}.\label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
Displayed group values compare $A_{g,t}$ with the same calibration maxima and
are labeled scan-adjusted. Neither $p$ nor $1-p$ is a fault probability.
The statement is per issuance, never indefinite stream-wide control.

Sensor participation averages group scores with inverse-size weights. A
development-only budget in $\{6,12,24\}$ selects the highest-ranked sensors;
participation is not causal attribution. Candidate oracle overlap measures
the library's localization ceiling. Display merging uses group Jaccard overlap
at least .5, retaining original records.

\section{Properties and Limits}
\begin{proposition}
For PSD correlation matrices, $0\leq H\leq1$. At zero shrinkage, identity and
rank-one matrices attain 1 and 0. With positive shrinkage the minimum is the
entropy of $(1-\lambda+\lambda/m,\lambda/m,\ldots,\lambda/m)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The nonnegative eigenvalues sum to $m$, hence define a probability vector.
Shannon's bound gives the first statement. Shrinkage gives every probability
at least $\lambda/m$; the displayed most-concentrated vector majorizes all
feasible vectors, and entropy is Schur-concave.
\end{proof}
For $n$ centered observations, rank is at most $\min(m,n-1)$, so zero-shrinkage
entropy is at most $\log\min(m,n-1)/\log m$. Autocorrelation further reduces
effective information without changing this algebraic ceiling; normalization
does not equalize finite-sample distributions across groups or windows.

\begin{proposition}
For $\R(\rho)=(1-\rho)I+\rho\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top$, $0\leq\rho<1$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dH}{d\rho}=\frac{m-1}{m\log m}
 \log\frac{1-\rho}{1+(m-1)\rho}\leq0.\label{eq:derivative}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues are $1+(m-1)\rho$ along $\mathbf1$ and $1-\rho$ on its
orthogonal complement. Differentiate their normalized entropy; constant
derivative terms cancel because the probabilities sum to one. For shrinkage,
replace $\rho$ by $(1-\lambda)\rho$ and multiply by $1-\lambda$.
\end{proof}
This is a statement about equicorrelation, not a universal law of faults.

For $m=4$, equicorrelation .2 has spectrum $(1.6,.8,.8,.8)$; two separate
correlated pairs at .6 have spectrum $(1.6,1.6,.4,.4)$. Both matrices are
positive definite, with mean pairwise correlation .2 and leading concentration
.4. Yet their zero-shrinkage entropies are .960964 and .860964
(Fig.~\ref{fig:theory}). Entropy distinguishes these organizations lost by
those simple summaries. It cannot contain more information than full $\R$.

\begin{proposition}
For an exchangeable set of calibration and null-test maxima from a fixed
scoring rule, (\ref{eq:rank}) satisfies $\Pr(p\leq\alpha)\leq\alpha$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under random tie breaking, symmetry makes the test rank among $n+1$ scores
uniform. Greater-or-equal tie counting is conservative. Thus the exceedance
probability is at most $\lfloor\alpha(n+1)\rfloor/(n+1)$.
\end{proof}
Real sensor blocks are not automatically exchangeable. Gaps do not establish
this assumption; dependence, distribution shift and contamination require
empirical evaluation. The minimum attainable $p$ is $1/(n+1)$.

Counterexamples delimit interpretation. Diagonal sign changes $S\R S$ and
fixed-group permutations preserve the spectrum. Offsets and positive rescaling
preserve correlations. A coherent fault and a legitimate common driver can
share $H$. Flatlines make correlations undefined. The detector therefore
needs signed correlation evidence and separately evaluated raw/quality alerts.

\section{Data and Preregistered Evaluation}

The synthetic benchmark uses random geometric graphs and a stable process
$x_{t+1}=(.82I-.25L)x_t+Bf_t+.12\epsilon_t$, with $L$ divided by maximum
degree, documented daily/weekly signals, spatial factor loadings and latent
OU drivers. Spectral radii are checked below one. Entire simulations have
disjoint train, development, calibration and test seeds; the three node counts
are configurations of one benchmark, not three primary datasets.

Intel Lab~\cite{intel} retains timestamps, masks, coordinates and units.
Right-labeled two-minute bins take the last observed whole row, with no
interpolation. A coverage-only audit, before Intel training/test scoring,
excludes the terminal sustained availability collapse after March 23; the
complete recording is retained for the quality sensitivity. All 54 motes
remain. Primary channels are temperature and humidity, with separately audited
light/voltage. PEMS-BAY uses the original DCRNN release~\cite{dcrnn}, its road
distances and all 325 sensors. Its actual file spans January--June 2017; the
one-hour March 12 gap is explicitly masked. Zero values follow the release's
missing-value evaluation convention. Per-value upstream imputation lineage
is unavailable, a limitation of using the processed release.

Real recordings use chronological 60/15/10/15\% splits. Windows never cross
split or synthetic-episode boundaries. Median/IQR scaling is training-only.
Training-loss screening excludes standardized magnitudes above eight; an
unscreened sensitivity tests this choice. Unlabeled real training recordings
are nominal, not certified healthy.

The protocol was committed before full runs. All methods see identical
controlled injections into untouched held-out recordings. The real-data study
is \emph{semi-synthetic}. Twelve fault mechanisms span copying/replay collapse,
common interference, coupling loss/decorrelation, noise, delays, gain/offset
drift, flatline/dropout, anticorrelation, mixed changes, matched mean correlation
and marginal-preserving rearrangement. Severities .6/1, durations 12/48/96 and
connected/partly disconnected groups are balanced in a fixed 72-event grid.
Legitimate synchronized transitions and untouched recordings are controls.
Entropy directions are measured, not assigned from the mechanism name.
For direction strata, take the median within-event $\Delta H$ in the
predefined group with greatest overlap with the injected sensor set; truth
is used only for evaluation, never for scoring or selecting alarm thresholds.

The primary scan budget is $\alpha=.10$, with .05/.20 reported where attainable.
Alerts are consecutive flagged issuance runs, with stride 12. Their onsets
must match true event intervals one-to-one, with zero trailing tolerance;
pre-existing alarms receive no credit. No point adjustment is used. Event
PR area integrates the upper precision envelope over attainable one-to-one
recalls and retains the raw curve. Localization scores missed events as zero,
with conditional detected-event results also available. Delays include window
and stride effects; measured execution latency is reported separately.

Resampling uses whole independent synthetic simulations or disjoint real
recording blocks, retaining all injections and averaging training seeds within
each block. We report paired 95\% bootstrap intervals, type macro scores,
prevalence and missed counts. Overlapping windows are not replicates. Native
background alerts are not verified false physical-fault detections.

\section{Auditable Monitoring and Limitations}
The saved replay links a stable spatial node/edge view, ranked groups, signed
residual heatmap and synchronized raw, entropy and non-entropic traces.
Blue/purple down markers and orange/red up markers label actual $\Delta H$;
higher/lower than the generated expectation is shown separately. Gray denotes
inadequate support and a distinct symbol identifies flatline quality status.
Inspection exposes group membership, common-row counts, units, observed and
reference correlations, shared-channel evidence and its support, checkpoint,
forecast issuance, observation time, window and scan-adjusted value. Physical
edges and correlation overlays are distinct. Reference bands use the same
samples as all compared traces. Trailing residual clustering is descriptive,
uses only past/current observations, and introduces no additional significance
claim. No firmware, manufacturer, maintenance or road-segment metadata are
invented; spatial proximity is an input, not a discovered cause.

Several limitations matter for deployment. Spectral compression loses sign,
sensor identity within a fixed group and other covariance information. Selection
of groups limits localization, while common-case deletion can make a large scan
ineligible. The Gaussian PSD sensitivity makes additional missingness/model
assumptions and does not silently replace the primary estimator. Forecast
misspecification or contaminated history can normalize persistent faults.
Gapped real calibration units still exhibit dependence, and small calibration
sets limit attainable tail levels. Synthetic mechanisms and real-data injections
do not establish accuracy on confirmed field failures. The dashboard is an
evidence-inspection artifact; no operator-usability improvement is claimed.
