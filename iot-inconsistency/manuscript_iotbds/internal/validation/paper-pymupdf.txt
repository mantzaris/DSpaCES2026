 Evaluating Graph-Conditioned Generative AI for IoT
          Sensor Fault Detection and Repair

                        Anonymous Author(s)


       Abstract. A temperature sensor reporting a sudden rise may indicate overheating
        or a faulty reading. Operators need to identify which measurements to rely on. We
        present a graph-conditioned generative AI method for locating inconsistent readings
       and proposing service. A neural normalizing ﬂow uses sensor history and related
       measurements to generate plausible trajectories on GPUs. The method compares
       normal readings with explicit bias, drift, noise and stuck-sensor explanations,
       producing fault scores and weighted repair distributions. Controlled evaluation on
       a synthetic family, Intel Berkeley and SKAB separates detection, conﬁdence and
        repair quality. The score improves mean average precision over PCA and even over
        the same ﬂow’s likelihood, with both paired primary intervals above zero. The
        smaller gain misses the declared practical margin, beneﬁts over matched Gaussian
      models remain inconclusive, and supervised trees perform better. Calibration
      and repair failures restrict operational claims. Versioned graph evidence and an
       annotated sensor network support human views. This research provides an audited,
       reproducible framework for assessing generative fault diagnosis, identifying
        detection gains and exposing reliability limitations that anomaly rankings alone
      would not reveal.

      Keywords: sensor data quality  · conditional generation  · fault localization  ·
        likelihood ratio · repair uncertainty

1  Introduction

Distributed sensors provide the measurements used by digital twins and shared data
spaces. When several temperature sensors rise together, they may be reporting a genuine
process change. Conversely, a sensor stuck at an ordinary value may be faulty. Operators
therefore need more than an anomaly alert, they need to identify questionable readings
and assess whether proposed corrections are needed.
  We use generative AI to compare two explanations for each sensor interval, the
recorded measurements are plausible, or a measurement fault has altered them. A
graph conditioned ﬂow generates possible reference trajectories from sensor history
and related measurements. Evaluating these trajectories under speciﬁed corruption
mechanisms produces a likelihood-ratio fault score and weighted repair distribution.
Supporting sensors provide context, graph edges describe predictive associations without
establishing causation.
  H1 tests whether integrating these generated explanations improves sensor-interval
average precision over development selected PCA and ordinary negative log likelihood
from the same ﬂow. H2 compares neural and Gaussian or probabilistic PCA models using
identical corruption assumptions. H3 evaluates useful repair coverage under a separately
selected policy aiming at a controlled failure rate. Repair is a secondary endpoint.
   The results support H1 in the three-family comparison while exposing limitations
in conﬁdence and repair reliability. The methodological value lies in separating these
outcomes through matched baselines, numerical audits and tests of missing targets,
unseen faults and corrupted context. The previous diﬀusion method is rerun on shared
cases. Fresh synthetic trajectories provide new tests, injections into previously examined
real recordings assess controlled fault behavior without establishing generalization to
untouched situations.

2  Related Work

PCA residuals are established monitoring tools [8]. Probabilistic PCA and its mixture
extension supply normalized distributions, generation and uncertainty [15,14]. Neural
beneﬁts must therefore be measured against these probabilistic repair alternatives. GANF
combines recurrence, learned graphs and ﬂows for anomaly detection [3]. MTGFlow
studies dynamic graphs and entity-speciﬁc densities [19]. Recent work examines de-
partures in conditional-ﬂow latent dynamics [1]. We use unchanged author GANF
classes with documented evaluation adapters. Our aﬃne couplings follow established
constructions [5]. Neither graph conditioning nor invertibility is new here.
   Likelihood ratios for distributional anomalies have substantial precedent [12].
Bayesian sensor diagnosis predates neural ﬂows [11], and particle ﬁltering integrates
uncertain latent system states when assessing sensor fault explanations [17]. A ﬂow
prior combined with a structured noise likelihood has also been used for inverse recov-
ery [16] (these are close predecessors). Our study evaluates a particular combination of
masked graph context, normalized short block corruption channels, marginal likelihood
comparison and weighted repair.
   CSDI and PriSTI generate conditional imputations [13,10]. ImDiﬀusion uses diﬀusion
imputation for anomaly detection [2], and GDN uses graph based forecast deviations [4].
We retain GDN and documented diﬀusion comparators. The earlier procedure tested
repairs against excluded witness measurements. The present conditional likelihood
method does not inherit that independence claim. A source comparison matrix records
implementation diﬀerences.

4.3  Limits of fault identiﬁcation
Two sensors can agree and still leave the cause uncertain. If both measure 푥푡, a
genuine process change of 푎푡produces (푥푡+ 푎푡, 푥푡+ 푎푡). The same readings arise if theprocess stays unchanged but both sensors acquire an additive fault 푎푡. With identical
available observations and context, no detector can distinguish these explanations without
additional information. Our stress study stores both scenarios with identical observations
and diﬀerent latent truth. Their ambiguity is known from construction, labeling these
cases does not establish that the detector recognizes ambiguity immediately.

5  Experimental Protocol

5.1  Datasets, independence and candidates
Table 1 summarizes the evaluated sensor intervals. S32L, S32N and S64N are 32 channel
linear, 32 channel nonlinear and 64 channel nonlinear conﬁgurations of one coupled
simulator, not independent datasets. New test trajectories separately record the physical
process, uncorrupted noisy measurements and injected faults. The last two of twelve ﬁnal
trajectories use the predeﬁned operating regime change.
    Intel provides 48 channels from twelve sensor nodes measuring temperature, humidity,
light and voltage [7]. Completed ﬁve minute bins retain missing values without interpola-
tion, chronological splits and gaps are preserved. SKAB has eight sensor/process channels
split by experiment [9]. Native process events are evaluated separately, controlled faults
use only reference windows without these events. Scaling uses training data, and physical
source groups remain static.
    All real partitions informed the earlier study, new injections therefore test familiar
recordings, not untouched environments. Test observations remain excluded from training.
Earlier synthetic training, development and calibration sources are reused, preserving the
previous comparator’s training opportunity.
   Each reference window, sampled by source block, produces one unchanged copy and
four fault copies. Development and calibration use bias, drift, excess noise and stuck
readings. Each ﬁnal reference receives two known family faults and two ’held out family
faults’, the latter rotate across spike, scale, replay and delay. Severities are newly sampled
from the ﬁxed range. Fault labels require a change in an observed target value. Wholly
unobservable injections are recorded as availability outcomes. Primary injections leave
other physical sources unchanged, but their original recordings may still contain faults.

or labeled fault examples. The ﬂow grid combines width/layer pairs (32, 4) and (64, 6)with context lengths 32 and 56. Each ﬁt uses 600 AdamW steps, batches of 128, learning
rate 0.001 and weight decay 0.0001. Normal development likelihood selects checkpoints
and capacity. Three ﬁxed seeds form one density mixture ensemble, not three ensemble
repetitions. S32N and SKAB learning curves use one quarter, one half and all training
blocks, (Table 7 reports model and training sizes).

value of explicit fault explanations. Its smaller gain remains below the chosen 0.02
practical margin. Improvements over PCA extend to S64N and both real data families,
with SKAB contributing most of the macro gain. This identiﬁes settings where the

7  Graph Evidence and Operator Review


Versioned Neo4j records make detection and repair evidence traceable across sensors,
physical sources, windows, associations, models, fault hypotheses, generations, scores
and repairs. Large arrays use content addressed ﬁles. Observations remain immutable,
accept, reject and defer decisions are stored separately without automatically applying

8  Discussion

Generated alternatives connect fault detection to proposed corrections and inspectable
graph evidence. Across three dataset families, macro AP increases by 0.131 over PCA
and 0.015 over same-ﬂow likelihood, with both primary intervals above zero. Supporting
sensors improve detection over own history alone. SKAB drives most of the PCA gain,
and the likelihood gain remains below the 0.02 practical margin.
    Separate evaluation of ranking, conﬁdence and repair shows where detection gains
support useful actions. Synthetic repairs are beneﬁcial without observed failures, but
poor Intel calibration and harmful SKAB recommendations prevent dependable de-
ployment claims. Matched Gaussian and mean-replacement controls leave neural and
integration beneﬁts unresolved; supervised trees perform better with labeled faults. These
comparisons guide conﬁdence calibration, repair selection and abstention.
   The ﬁndings concern controlled faults in short, complete intervals. Missing measure-
ments, simultaneous failures, irregular timing and process-versus-fault ambiguity limit
generalization.

9  Conclusion

This work connects generative AI to a complete sensor-review workﬂow, generating
plausible readings, testing fault explanations, proposing corrections and presenting
traceable graph evidence. The evaluation establishes detection gains over PCA and
same-model likelihood while identifying remaining conﬁdence and repair challenges.
Its contribution combines measured improvements with a reproducible framework for
investigating when generative sensor diagnosis can support trustworthy decisions.
