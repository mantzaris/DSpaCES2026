Evaluating Graph-Conditioned Generative AI for IoT
Sensor Fault Detection and Repair
Anonymous Author(s)

Abstract. A temperature sensor reporting a sudden rise may indicate overheating
or a faulty reading. Operators need to identify which measurements to rely on. We
present a graph-conditioned generative AI method for locating inconsistent readings
and proposing service. A neural normalizing flow uses sensor history and related
measurements to generate plausible trajectories on GPUs. The method compares
normal readings with explicit bias, drift, noise and stuck-sensor explanations,
producing fault scores and weighted repair distributions. Controlled evaluation on
a synthetic family, Intel Berkeley and SKAB separates detection, confidence and
repair quality. The score improves mean average precision over PCA and even over
the same flow’s likelihood, with both paired primary intervals above zero. The
smaller gain misses the declared practical margin, benefits over matched Gaussian
models remain inconclusive, and supervised trees perform better. Calibration
and repair failures restrict operational claims. Versioned graph evidence and an
annotated sensor network support human views. This research provides an audited,
reproducible framework for assessing generative fault diagnosis, identifying
detection gains and exposing reliability limitations that anomaly rankings alone
would not reveal.
Keywords: sensor data quality · conditional generation · fault localization ·
likelihood ratio · repair uncertainty

1

Introduction

Distributed sensors provide the measurements used by digital twins and shared data
spaces. When several temperature sensors rise together, they may be reporting a genuine
process change. Conversely, a sensor stuck at an ordinary value may be faulty. Operators
therefore need more than an anomaly alert, they need to identify questionable readings
and assess whether proposed corrections are needed.
We use generative AI to compare two explanations for each sensor interval, the
recorded measurements are plausible, or a measurement fault has altered them. A
graph conditioned flow generates possible reference trajectories from sensor history
and related measurements. Evaluating these trajectories under specified corruption
mechanisms produces a likelihood-ratio fault score and weighted repair distribution.
Supporting sensors provide context, graph edges describe predictive associations without
establishing causation.
H1 tests whether integrating these generated explanations improves sensor-interval
average precision over development selected PCA and ordinary negative log likelihood
from the same flow. H2 compares neural and Gaussian or probabilistic PCA models using

identical corruption assumptions. H3 evaluates useful repair coverage under a separately
selected policy aiming at a controlled failure rate. Repair is a secondary endpoint.
The results support H1 in the three-family comparison while exposing limitations
in confidence and repair reliability. The methodological value lies in separating these
outcomes through matched baselines, numerical audits and tests of missing targets,
unseen faults and corrupted context. The previous diffusion method is rerun on shared
cases. Fresh synthetic trajectories provide new tests, injections into previously examined
real recordings assess controlled fault behavior without establishing generalization to
untouched situations.

Related Work

PCA residuals are established monitoring tools [8]. Probabilistic PCA and its mixture
extension supply normalized distributions, generation and uncertainty [15,14]. Neural
benefits must therefore be measured against these probabilistic repair alternatives. GANF
combines recurrence, learned graphs and flows for anomaly detection [3]. MTGFlow
studies dynamic graphs and entity-specific densities [19]. Recent work examines departures in conditional-flow latent dynamics [1]. We use unchanged author GANF
classes with documented evaluation adapters. Our affine couplings follow established
constructions [5]. Neither graph conditioning nor invertibility is new here.
Likelihood ratios for distributional anomalies have substantial precedent [12].
Bayesian sensor diagnosis predates neural flows [11], and particle filtering integrates
uncertain latent system states when assessing sensor fault explanations [17]. A flow
prior combined with a structured noise likelihood has also been used for inverse recovery [16] (these are close predecessors). Our study evaluates a particular combination of
masked graph context, normalized short block corruption channels, marginal likelihood
comparison and weighted repair.
CSDI and PriSTI generate conditional imputations [13,10]. ImDiffusion uses diffusion
imputation for anomaly detection [2], and GDN uses graph based forecast deviations [4].
We retain GDN and documented diffusion comparators. The earlier procedure tested
repairs against excluded witness measurements. The present conditional likelihood
method does not inherit that independence claim. A source comparison matrix records
implementation differences.

3.1

Conditional Fault Explanations
Sensor intervals and supporting context

Each sensor’s recent readings are assessed against its history and related sensors. Every
32 steps, we examine a 64 step window and test each channel’s final eight observations.
Candidate ℎ identifies the target block, with values 𝑦 ℎ ∈ R𝐷ℎ and 𝐷 ℎ = 8. The variable
𝑧 ℎ represents a possible uncorrupted, but still noisy, version. All eight values must be
available. Incomplete blocks are flagged and assigned a common lowest score, retaining
their faults as misses (missing targets are not zeros).

The context 𝐶ℎ contains the information used to generate reference readings. To
prevent copying the tested measurements, we hide the same eight-step interval in every
channel from the target’s physical source before computing features or graph messages.
Earlier readings remain available. Training medians and interquartile scales normalize
channels, missing context is masked, and duplicate measurements are represented once.
With latent draws fixed, altering hidden values or copies must leave the context and
generated samples unchanged, the target likelihood may change.
The frozen graph 𝐺 identifies supporting channels through predictive associations
selected using training and development data. Edges record direction, lag, units, training
correlation and development support. Up to three incoming channels from distinct other
sources provide context. A shared gated recurrent unit summarizes histories, channel
embeddings identify sensors, and available graph messages are averaged. These inputs
form 𝑐 ℎ = Encoder 𝜓 (𝐶ℎ , 𝐺). Only information available at the decision time is used.
Lags and recurrent steps count observations, whose elapsed durations can differ in
irregular recordings.

4.3

Limits of fault identification

Two sensors can agree and still leave the cause uncertain. If both measure 𝑥 𝑡 , a
genuine process change of 𝑎 𝑡 produces (𝑥 𝑡 + 𝑎 𝑡 , 𝑥 𝑡 + 𝑎 𝑡 ). The same readings arise if the
process stays unchanged but both sensors acquire an additive fault 𝑎 𝑡 . With identical
available observations and context, no detector can distinguish these explanations without
additional information. Our stress study stores both scenarios with identical observations
and different latent truth. Their ambiguity is known from construction, labeling these
cases does not establish that the detector recognizes ambiguity immediately.

5.1

Experimental Protocol
Datasets, independence and candidates

Table 1 summarizes the evaluated sensor intervals. S32L, S32N and S64N are 32 channel
linear, 32 channel nonlinear and 64 channel nonlinear configurations of one coupled
simulator, not independent datasets. New test trajectories separately record the physical
process, uncorrupted noisy measurements and injected faults. The last two of twelve final
trajectories use the predefined operating regime change.
Intel provides 48 channels from twelve sensor nodes measuring temperature, humidity,
light and voltage [7]. Completed five minute bins retain missing values without interpolation, chronological splits and gaps are preserved. SKAB has eight sensor/process channels
split by experiment [9]. Native process events are evaluated separately, controlled faults
use only reference windows without these events. Scaling uses training data, and physical
source groups remain static.
All real partitions informed the earlier study, new injections therefore test familiar
recordings, not untouched environments. Test observations remain excluded from training.
Earlier synthetic training, development and calibration sources are reused, preserving the
previous comparator’s training opportunity.
Each reference window, sampled by source block, produces one unchanged copy and
four fault copies. Development and calibration use bias, drift, excess noise and stuck
readings. Each final reference receives two known family faults and two ’held out family
faults’, the latter rotate across spike, scale, replay and delay. Severities are newly sampled
from the fixed range. Fault labels require a change in an observed target value. Wholly
unobservable injections are recorded as availability outcomes. Primary injections leave
other physical sources unchanged, but their original recordings may still contain faults.

or labeled fault examples. The flow grid combines width/layer pairs (32, 4) and (64, 6)

rate 0.001 and weight decay 0.0001. Normal development likelihood selects checkpoints
and capacity. Three fixed seeds form one density mixture ensemble, not three ensemble
repetitions. S32N and SKAB learning curves use one quarter, one half and all training
blocks, (Table 7 reports model and training sizes).

The audits expose numerical limits alongside these gains. Even at 2048 draws per
member, SKAB’s 95th percentile repeated seed score change is 0.952, so this finite
reference does not establish exact integration. Low-ESS cases retain detection scores but
abstain from repair. Missing support affects attribution (Table 6). With multiple faults,
selecting another faulty sensor can lower original target top one accuracy without being
incorrect. When half the other S32N sources are corrupted, AP across all true targets
is 0.946, under the changed fault prevalence. Canonicalizing known copies preserves
scores exactly in every registered copy case.
Direct residuals provide a separate association diagnostic, achieving mean edge
localization AP of 0.977 across twelve S32N cases with half the associations changed.
Reading and association alarms can coincide or both remain silent. Cross type counts are
saved; reading alarms do not prove false edges. The physical change counterexample
clarifies an unavoidable ambiguity, identical readings can reflect a process change or
shared sensor faults. All twelve S32N physical change cases trigger reading alarms at
0.10. On 224 native SKAB windows, including 78 process-event windows, AP is 0.536
for the ratio, 0.546 for NLL and 0.499 for PCA. These results assess process detection,
not sensor fault diagnosis.

6.4

Numerical evidence and computation

The implementation passes eight scalar-reference check groups and forty local tests.
Eighteen flow checks cover inverse/Jacobian agreement, Gaussian integrals and posteriors,
normalized corruption, units, mixture arithmetic, masking, missingness, source copies,
chunking and repair damage. A GPU pilot trained a 22,048-parameter flow on Gaussian
simulations, changing validation NLL from 10.669 to 5.722 in 9.69 seconds.
The production audit reconstructs 107,620 calibration and final scores from saved
component integrals, with maximum discrepancy 9.09 × 10−13 . Independent NumPy
corruption densities and GPU model/draw replay check 30 full generation bundles.
Maximum generation, normal density, corruption density and ratio discrepancies are 0,
0, 9.12 × 10−12 and 3.95 × 10−12 . Weighted posterior means agree within 1.97 × 10−12 ;
analytic and autograd Jacobian determinants agree within 3.55×10−15 for a trained model.
Together, these checks link reported scores and repairs to the implemented equations,
predictive quality is assessed through the benchmarks.

Graph Evidence and Operator Review

Versioned Neo4j records make detection and repair evidence traceable across sensors,
physical sources, windows, associations, models, fault hypotheses, generations, scores
and repairs. Large arrays use content addressed files. Observations remain immutable,
accept, reject and defer decisions are stored separately without automatically applying
repairs.
A conventional 2-dimensional network presents named sensors and directed edges
annotated with lag and training correlation. Red outlines identify disputed intervals, blue
outlines supporting sources, and amber dashed edges separately assessed association
disputes. Details show observed and generated trajectories, weighted repair intervals, ESS,
benchmark probability and abstention. Reading scores do not establish per-edge certainty.
The 23 saved cases cover first correct and incorrect attributions, inadequate sampling,
constructed ambiguity and association review. Repeated Neo4j exports preserve entity
counts. Automated browser checks verify evidence loading, node/edge selection, generated
curves, hidden-by-default evaluation truth, artifact downloads, decision persistence and
unchanged observations. These checks provide reproducible validation of the review
interface; operator effectiveness remains to be evaluated.

8

Discussion

Generated alternatives connect fault detection to proposed corrections and inspectable
graph evidence. Across three dataset families, macro AP increases by 0.131 over PCA
and 0.015 over same-flow likelihood, with both primary intervals above zero. Supporting
sensors improve detection over own history alone. SKAB drives most of the PCA gain,
and the likelihood gain remains below the 0.02 practical margin.
Separate evaluation of ranking, confidence and repair shows where detection gains
support useful actions. Synthetic repairs are beneficial without observed failures, but
poor Intel calibration and harmful SKAB recommendations prevent dependable deployment claims. Matched Gaussian and mean-replacement controls leave neural and
integration benefits unresolved; supervised trees perform better with labeled faults. These
comparisons guide confidence calibration, repair selection and abstention.
The findings concern controlled faults in short, complete intervals. Missing measurements, simultaneous failures, irregular timing and process-versus-fault ambiguity limit
generalization.

9

Conclusion

This work connects generative AI to a complete sensor-review workflow, generating
plausible readings, testing fault explanations, proposing corrections and presenting
traceable graph evidence. The evaluation establishes detection gains over PCA and
same-model likelihood while identifying remaining confidence and repair challenges.
Its contribution combines measured improvements with a reproducible framework for
investigating when generative sensor diagnosis can support trustworthy decisions.
