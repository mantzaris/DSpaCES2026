\begin{abstract}
A temperature sensor reporting a sudden rise may indicate overheating or a faulty reading. Operators need to identify which measurements to rely on. We present a graph-conditioned generative AI method for locating inconsistent readings and proposing service. A neural normalizing flow uses sensor history and related measurements to generate plausible trajectories on GPUs. The method compares normal readings with explicit bias, drift, noise and stuck-sensor explanations, producing fault scores and weighted repair distributions. Controlled evaluation on a synthetic family, Intel Berkeley and SKAB separates detection, confidence and repair quality. The score improves mean average precision over PCA and even over the same flow's likelihood, with both paired primary intervals above zero. The smaller gain misses the declared practical margin, benefits over matched Gaussian models remain inconclusive, and supervised trees perform better. Calibration and repair failures restrict operational claims. Versioned graph evidence and an annotated sensor network support human views. This research provides an audited, reproducible framework for assessing generative fault diagnosis, identifying detection gains and exposing reliability limitations that anomaly rankings alone would not reveal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Distributed sensors provide the measurements used by digital twins and shared data spaces. When several temperature sensors rise together, they may be reporting a genuine process change. Conversely, a sensor stuck at an ordinary value may be faulty. Operators therefore need more than an anomaly alert, they need to identify questionable readings and assess whether proposed corrections are needed.

We use generative AI to compare two explanations for each sensor interval, the recorded measurements are plausible, or a measurement fault has altered them. A graph conditioned flow generates possible reference trajectories from sensor history and related measurements. Evaluating these trajectories under specified corruption mechanisms produces a likelihood-ratio fault score and weighted repair distribution. Supporting sensors provide context, graph edges describe predictive associations without establishing causation.

H1 tests whether integrating these generated explanations improves sensor-interval average precision over development selected PCA and ordinary negative log likelihood from the same flow. H2 compares neural and Gaussian or probabilistic PCA models using identical corruption assumptions. H3 evaluates useful repair coverage under a separately selected policy aiming at a controlled failure rate. Repair is a secondary endpoint.

The results support H1 in the three-family comparison while exposing limitations in confidence and repair reliability. The methodological value lies in separating these outcomes through matched baselines, numerical audits and tests of missing targets, unseen faults and corrupted context. The previous diffusion method is rerun on shared cases. Fresh synthetic trajectories provide new tests, injections into previously examined real recordings assess controlled fault behavior without establishing generalization to untouched situations.



\section{Related Work}
PCA residuals are established monitoring tools~\cite{pca}. Probabilistic PCA and its mixture extension supply normalized distributions, generation and uncertainty~\cite{ppca,mppca}. Neural benefits must therefore be measured against these probabilistic repair alternatives.
GANF combines recurrence, learned graphs and flows for anomaly detection~\cite{ganf}. MTGFlow studies dynamic graphs and entity-specific densities~\cite{mtgflow}. Recent work examines departures in conditional-flow latent dynamics~\cite{latentflow}. We use unchanged author GANF classes with documented evaluation adapters. Our affine couplings follow established constructions~\cite{realnvp}. Neither graph conditioning nor invertibility is new here.

Likelihood ratios for distributional anomalies have substantial precedent~\cite{ratio}. Bayesian sensor diagnosis predates neural flows~\cite{bayesfault}, and particle filtering integrates uncertain latent system states when assessing sensor fault explanations~\cite{wright}. A flow prior combined with a structured noise likelihood has also been used for inverse recovery~\cite{whang} (these are close  predecessors). Our study evaluates a particular combination of masked graph context, normalized short block corruption channels, marginal likelihood comparison and weighted repair. 

CSDI and PriSTI generate conditional imputations~\cite{csdi,pristi}. ImDiffusion uses diffusion imputation for anomaly detection~\cite{imdiffusion}, and GDN uses graph based forecast deviations~\cite{gdn}. We retain GDN and documented diffusion comparators. Our earlier procedure tested repairs against excluded witness measurements. The present conditional likelihood method does not inherit that independence claim. A source comparison matrix records implementation differences.





\section{Conditional Fault Explanations}

\subsection{Sensor intervals and supporting context}
Each sensor's recent readings are assessed against its history and related sensors. Every 32 steps, we examine a 64 step window and test each channel's final eight observations. Candidate $h$ identifies the target block, with values $y_h\in\mathbb R^{D_h}$ and $D_h=8$. The variable $z_h$ represents a possible uncorrupted, but still noisy, version. All eight values must be available. Incomplete blocks are flagged and assigned a common lowest score, retaining their faults as misses (missing targets are not zeros).

The context $C_h$ contains the information used to generate reference readings. To prevent copying the tested measurements, we hide the same eight-step interval in every channel from the target's physical source before computing features or graph messages. Earlier readings remain available. Training medians and interquartile scales normalize channels, missing context is masked, and duplicate measurements are represented once. With latent draws fixed, altering hidden values or copies must leave the context and generated samples unchanged, the target likelihood may change.

The frozen graph $G$ identifies supporting channels through predictive associations selected using training and development data. Edges record direction, lag, units, training correlation and development support. Up to three incoming channels from distinct other sources provide context. A shared gated recurrent unit summarizes histories, channel embeddings identify sensors, and available graph messages are averaged. These inputs form $c_h=\operatorname{Encoder}_{\psi}(C_h,G)$. Only information available at the decision time is used. Lags and recurrent steps count observations, whose elapsed durations can differ in irregular recordings.


\subsection{Neural density and actual generation}
An invertible conditional flow maps a target block to a standard normal latent vector. With $f_\theta$ denoting the forward map,
\begin{equation}
 \log p_\theta(y_h\mid c_h)=\log p_0(f_\theta(y_h;c_h))
 +\log\left|\det\frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial y_h}\right|.
\end{equation}
The conditioner is fixed when this target Jacobian is evaluated. Alternating affine couplings have triangular Jacobians and bounded neural log scales. A conditional location and scale transform precedes them. Generation applies the inverse on the GPU,
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_h^{(m)}\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\qquad
 z_h^{(m)}=f_\theta^{-1}(\epsilon_h^{(m)};c_h).
\end{equation}
These are numerical sensor trajectories. They enter both the fault score and the repair calculation below. The reference training objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm density}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{h=1}^{N}\log p_\theta(y_h\mid c_h).
\end{equation}
Reference denotes the declared training population, not certified correct instrumentation. Saved records contain masks, contexts, latent draws, generated intervals and model hashes for representative production cases.

\subsection{Normalized corruption channels}
The normal explanation uses the flow density directly,
\begin{equation}
 p_{{\rm normal},h}(y_h\mid C_h,G)=p_\theta(y_h\mid c_h).
\end{equation}
The fault explanation uses a normalized mixture
\begin{equation}
 q_\phi(y\mid z)=\sum_{k=1}^{4}\pi_k\mathcal N(y;H_kz,R_k),
 \qquad \pi_k=\tfrac14.
\end{equation}
For bias, drift and excess noise, $H_k=I$. For a stuck reading, $H_k=\1 e_1^T$, so the uncertain stuck level is centered on the first generated reference value. Let $s\in\{1,2\}$ be the development-selected scale and $r=0.25$ the fixed noise floor. The covariances are
\[
\begin{split}
 R_{\rm bias}&=s^2\1\1^T+r^2I,\qquad
 R_{\rm drift}=s^2tt^T+r^2I,\\
 R_{\rm noise}&=s^2I,\qquad
 R_{\rm stuck}=s^2\1\1^T+r^2I.
\end{split}
\]
Here $t$ is the actual target timestamp ramp rescaled to $[-1,1]$. Bias and drift amplitudes are shared across the block. Independent amplitudes at every coordinate would be different models. Positive floors make each covariance positive definite. Cholesky based determinants and quadratic forms produce correct Gaussian densities. Dropout is an availability event and is not represented by a numeric corruption density.

The marginal fault density and log ratio are
\begin{align}
 p_{{\rm fault},h}(y_h\mid c_h)&=\int q_\phi(y_h\mid z)p_\theta(z\mid c_h)\,dz, \\
 S_h&=\log p_{{\rm fault},h}(y_h\mid c_h)-\log p_\theta(y_h\mid c_h).
\end{align}
Positive $S_h$ favors the declared fault model. It is not itself a calibrated probability or proof of sensor failure. With generated trajectories from (E2),
\begin{equation}
 \widehat S_h=\log\left[\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}q_\phi(y_h\mid z_h^{(m)})\right]
 -\log p_\theta(y_h\mid c_h).
\end{equation}
We use stable log-sum-exp arithmetic. The logarithm of the average density is essential. Averaging log densities or taking the best generation does not implement this integral. The density estimate is unbiased under independent prior sampling, but its logarithm generally has downward bias.

For $E$ equally weighted trained models with $M$ samples each,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \widehat S_{h,E}={}&\LME_{e,m}\bigl[\log q_\phi(y_h\mid z_h^{(e,m)})\bigr]\\
 &-\LME_e\bigl[\log p_{\theta_e}(y_h\mid c_h)\bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\LME(a_1,\ldots,a_n)=\log(\sum_i\exp(a_i)/n)$. The ordinary flow comparator is the negative second term's underlying mixture log density, using exactly the same checkpoints. An average of member log ratios is a separately labeled ablation.





\subsection{Repair distribution and numerical adequacy}
Bayes' rule under the fault explanation gives
\begin{equation}
 p(z\mid y_h,c_h,H_{1,h})=
 \frac{q_\phi(y_h\mid z)p_\theta(z\mid c_h)}{p_{{\rm fault},h}(y_h\mid c_h)}.
\end{equation}
For a uniform ensemble, we normalize $q_\phi(y_h\mid z_h^{(e,m)})$ over all generated members and samples to obtain weights $w_{e,m}$. Weighted means evaluate squared-error repair, and weighted medians and marginal quantiles are also kept. The reference truth never chooses a sample. A mean need not preserve a physical mode.
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{ESS}_h=\left(\sum_{e,m}w_{e,m}^2\right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
An ESS below 16 prevents a repair recommendation but leaves the detection score in the evaluated population. Low ESS identifies poor importance sampling resolution, not necessarily large physical uncertainty. Repeated sampling seeds and a finite draw grid diagnose this limitation separately from differences between trained models.

We apply a proposed action to the full evaluated window. With normalized squared loss $L$ summed over observed cells and divided by eight, reference $y_j^\star$, acceptance $a_j$ and useful-improvement threshold $\delta=0.01$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \Delta_j&=L(y_j,y_j^\star)-L(\operatorname{repair}_j,y_j^\star),\\
 F_j&=\1\{\text{wrong target}\ \text{or}\ \Delta_j\leq\delta\},\\
 \operatorname{Coverage}&=\frac{\sum_j a_j}{n},\quad
 \operatorname{Risk}=\frac{\sum_j a_jF_j}{\sum_j a_j}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
A 'wrong target action' includes damage to previously correct measurements. Harmful edits have $\Delta_j<0$. No accepted action gives zero coverage and undefined risk. On real datasets the repair reference is the pre-injection recording, not unknown physical truth.





\section{Mathematical Checks and Limits}

\subsection{Probability checks and false alarms}
To compare normal and faulty readings, both explanations must assign total probability one to the same target space. In (E6), the nonnegative integrand allows us to integrate over $y$ first. Each corruption channel integrates to one, leaving $\int p_\theta(z\mid c)\,dz=1$. The resulting fault model is therefore a valid density. Both explanations use the same continuous approximation for quantized readings.
Suppose the normal model exactly describes normal measurements and the fault model assigns no probability to regions excluded by the normal model. Then
\[
 \E_{H_0}[\exp(S_h)\mid c_h]
 =\int p_{\rm fault}(y\mid c_h)\,dy=1.
\]
Markov's inequality gives
$\Pr_{H_0}(S_h\geq\log(1/\alpha)\mid c_h)\leq\alpha$:
under these assumptions, a fixed candidate exceeds this threshold with probability at most $\alpha$. Learned models can violate the assumptions through density error, corrupted context or distribution shift. Searching across many candidates also increases opportunities for false alarms, so the largest score does not inherit this bound. Averaging valid ratios with fixed nonnegative weights summing to one preserves the expectation, observation dependent weights require separate justification. We therefore calibrate the actual maximum score used by the alarm procedure.

\subsection{Gaussian reference and measurement units}
A Gaussian example provides an exact answer against which to check the implementation. Let reference readings follow $z\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$ and let independent corruption add $\eta\sim\mathcal N(0,R)$. The recorded values $y=z+\eta$ then follow $\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma+R)$. Comparing this fault density with the normal density gives
\[
 S=-\tfrac12\log\frac{|\Sigma+R|}{|\Sigma|}
 +\tfrac12(y-\mu)^T
 [\Sigma^{-1}-(\Sigma+R)^{-1}](y-\mu).
\]
The corresponding repair distribution has mean
$\mu+\Sigma(\Sigma+R)^{-1}(y-\mu)$
and covariance
$\Sigma-\Sigma(\Sigma+R)^{-1}\Sigma$.
For the affine channels in (E5), the fault density instead uses mean $H_k\mu$ and covariance $H_k\Sigma H_k^T+R_k$. Conditional PPCA evaluates these Gaussian integrals and posterior mixture weights exactly, providing a baseline with the same fault assumptions but without neural sampling.

Changing units should not change the score when both models are transformed consistently. Under an invertible change $u=g(y)$, both densities acquire the factor $|\det Dg^{-1}(u)|$, which cancels in their ratio. This property concerns the same distributions described differently, retraining models or changing corruption assumptions can change the results. Numerical checks include a non-diagonal affine transformations.

\subsection{Limits of fault identification}
Two sensors can agree and still leave the cause uncertain. If both measure $x_t$, a genuine process change of $a_t$ produces $(x_t+a_t,x_t+a_t)$. The same readings arise if the process stays unchanged but both sensors acquire an additive fault $a_t$. With identical available observations and context, no detector can distinguish these explanations without additional information. Our stress study stores both scenarios with identical observations and different latent truth. Their ambiguity is known from construction, labeling these cases does not establish that the detector recognizes ambiguity immediately.

\subsection{Development selection and comparators}
Matched comparisons test what drives detection gains, fault scoring, neural modeling or labeled fault examples. The flow grid combines width/layer pairs $(32,4)$ and $(64,6)$ with context lengths 32 and 56. Each fit uses 600 AdamW steps, batches of 128, learning rate 0.001 and weight decay 0.0001. Normal development likelihood selects checkpoints and capacity. Three fixed seeds form one density mixture ensemble, not three ensemble repetitions. S32N and SKAB learning curves use one quarter, one half and all training blocks, (Table~\ref{tab:runtime} reports model and training sizes).

Development selects corruption scale 1 or 2 and sampling budget $M\in\{32,128,512,2048\}$. We choose the smallest budget with a 95th-percentile absolute score difference at most 0.25 and rank correlation at least 0.98 against 2048 draws, defaulting to 2048 if needed. These checks assess numerical stability without guaranteeing exact integration. Separate sampling seeds test repeatability. All choices, data hashes and numerical source hashes are frozen before final scoring.

The identical flow's negative log likelihood isolates the scoring contribution. PCA compares current-only and four-lag reconstructions, with valid ranks from 2, 4, 8 and 16 selected by development candidate AP. Scores average target-channel squared residuals across eight steps, window maxima and full residual norms are stored separately. Conditional PPCA and two component mixture PPCA test neural modeling against Gaussian alternatives with identical graph context and $q_\phi$. Normal development likelihood selects ranks 2, 4 or 8. All channel PPCA is reported separately. Missing context is marginalized during evaluation but mean filled during fitting, a limitation when missingness is informative.

GANF uses the authors' unchanged recurrent, graph and autoregressive flow classes at a pinned revision, widths 16 and 32, ten graph optimization iterations and forty steps per iteration. The adapter excludes missing target losses and same source adjacency, aggregating the final eight coordinate densities. GANF can use preceding target readings within that interval and fills missing context without a mask. We report remaining acyclicity residuals rather than assuming exact convergence. MTGFlow was inspected but not run within budget.

A gradient boosted classifier tests the value of explicit fault labels, using shape, earlier history, PCA residual and availability features. It trains on the four development fault types injected into training windows.
The earlier diffusion witness, GDN, backbone residual and documented DiffAD adaptation~\cite{diffad} are rerun on shared manifests.
The witness retains its top-four screen, ignored candidates receive a score floor, keeping excluded faults as misses. Earlier window level results remain archived separately.




\subsection{Confidence and decision policy}
Three disjoint sets of whole calibration blocks separate probability estimation, alarm calibration and repair selection. Labeled blocks fit each comparator's logistic probability calibration using a robustly scaled score and scorable indicator. Normal blocks calibrate each window's maximum candidate score. For $n$ normal calibration windows,
\[
 p_{\rm window}=\frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{n}\1\{S_{\max,b}\geq S_{\max,{\rm new}}\}}{n+1}.
\]
The minimum $1/(n+1)$ limits attainable alarm levels; dependence and distribution shift further restrict guarantees.

Remaining blocks select a repair probability threshold, maximizing coverage over a fixed grid subject to ten or more actions and a one-sided 95\% Wilson failure upper bound $\leq0.10$. If none qualifies, the policy abstains. Under dependence, this is a calibration diagnostic, not a deployment guarantee. Test failures and harmful repairs are reported. Marginal CRPS, interval coverage and width, and joint energy score assess repair distributions~\cite{crps}. Flow energy estimates use 512 weighted sample pairs.

\subsection{Endpoints and uncertainty}
Primary AP measures fault ranking across all sensor intervals, including clean windows and unaffected candidates. For comparator $b$, the registered contrast is
\[
 \Delta_b=\tfrac13\sum_{d\in\{\mathrm{synthetic,Intel,SKAB}\}}
       (\operatorname{AP}_{\rm ratio,d}-\operatorname{AP}_{b,d}),
\]
The three synthetic configurations are averaged first, giving each dataset family equal weight. The practical improvement margin is 0.02 absolute AP. Directional H1 requires positive evidence against both PCA and same flow NLL.

Uncertainty uses 1000 paired whole block bootstrap replicates, resampling synthetic trajectories, Intel time blocks and SKAB experiments while retaining correlated candidates and injected copies. Paired 32-channel synthetic seeds share draws. Individual 97.5\% intervals for the two primary contrasts support a Bonferroni simultaneous 95\% statement. Dataset intervals are descriptive, few real blocks limit stability. Training seeds are not independent test subjects. Secondary metrics assess localization, window detection, calibration, review precision, repairs and measured costs.

\subsection{Calibration, review and repair}
The ratio improves Brier score over PCA in all synthetic configurations and SKAB, supporting improved probability prediction on these datasets. Intel exposes a calibration limit: Brier score is 0.068 with block interval [0.054, 0.082], versus PCA's 0.017. Its minimum window p-value of 0.059 cannot resolve 1\% or 5\% alarm alpha levels. At level 0.10, 79 of 82 unchanged cases alarm, so improved AP does not establish usable Intel alarms. Normal cases include injections that changed no observed coordinate.

Synthetic repairs provide a positive result, the flow policy accepts useful corrections without observed failures. S64N accepts 125 of 320 cases, compared with no PCA recommendations and 129 for PPCA. PPCA overall achieves greater coverage. Zero observed failures does not establish zero population risk, particularly with dependent blocks. Intel abstains throughout, leaving recommendation risk undefined.

On SKAB, 123 accepted repairs has 24 harmful failures, risk 0.195 with block interval [0.000, 0.400], versus 0.371 for PCA and 0.183 for PPCA. Observed risk is lower than PCA's, but exceeds the calibration target; H3 remains unestablished. Matched PPCA also has lower CRPS and energy score throughout (Table~\ref{tab:repair}), while wider flow intervals do not ensure reliable real-data coverage. Successful and failed examples (Figure~\ref{fig:repair}) make these trade-offs inspectable. The framework therefore distinguishes detection gains from repair quality and decision reliability. Calibrated probabilities reflect the designed injection prevalence, not operational physical failure probabilities.

\subsection{Ablations and adverse conditions}
Graph context contributes a clear detection gain, macro AP improves by 0.053 [+0.028, +0.077] over own history alone. Integration adds 0.003 [-0.007, +0.020] over replacing generated references with their prior mean, leaving its additional benefit inconclusive. Family macro AP is 0.427 for one member and 0.468 when member log ratios is averaged instead of mixing densities. All 'leave one corruption channel out' results are retained without revising the fault prior after testing, scale sensitivity uses development results.

The audits expose numerical limits alongside these gains. Even at 2048 draws per member, SKAB's 95th percentile repeated seed score change is 0.952, so this finite reference does not establish exact integration. Low-ESS cases retain detection scores but abstain from repair. Missing support affects attribution (Table~\ref{tab:stress}). With multiple faults, selecting another faulty sensor can lower original target top one accuracy without being incorrect. When half the other S32N sources are corrupted, AP across all true targets is 0.946, under the changed fault prevalence. Canonicalizing known copies preserves scores exactly in every registered copy case.

Direct residuals provide a separate association diagnostic, achieving mean edge localization AP of 0.977 across twelve S32N cases with half the associations changed. Reading and association alarms can coincide or both remain silent. Cross type counts are saved reading alarms do not prove false edges. The physical change counterexample clarifies an unavoidable ambiguity, identical readings can reflect a process change or shared sensor faults. All twelve S32N physical change cases trigger reading alarms at 0.10. On 224 native SKAB windows, including 78 process-event windows, AP is 0.536 for the ratio, 0.546 for NLL and 0.499 for PCA. These results assess process detection, not sensor fault diagnosis.

\section{Discussion}
Generated alternatives connect fault detection to proposed corrections and inspectable graph evidence. Across three dataset families, macro AP increases by 0.131 over PCA and 0.015 over same-flow likelihood, with both primary intervals above zero. Supporting sensors improve detection over own history alone. SKAB drives most of the PCA gain, and the likelihood gain remains below the 0.02 practical margin.

Separate evaluation of ranking, confidence and repair shows where detection gains support useful actions. Synthetic repairs are beneficial without observed failures, but poor Intel calibration and harmful SKAB recommendations prevent dependable deployment claims. Matched Gaussian and mean-replacement controls leave neural and integration benefits unresolved; supervised trees perform better with labeled faults. These comparisons guide confidence calibration, repair selection and abstention.

The findings concern controlled faults in short, complete intervals. Missing measurements, simultaneous failures, irregular timing and process-versus-fault ambiguity limit generalization.






\section{Conclusion}
This work connects generative AI to a complete sensor-review workflow, generating plausible readings, testing fault explanations, proposing corrections and presenting traceable graph evidence. The evaluation establishes detection gains over PCA and same-model likelihood while identifying remaining confidence and repair challenges. Its contribution combines measured improvements with a reproducible framework for investigating when generative sensor diagnosis can support trustworthy decisions.
