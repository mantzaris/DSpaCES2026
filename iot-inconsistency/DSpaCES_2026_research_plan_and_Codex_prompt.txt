PART A. RESEARCH SPECIFICATION

A1. Venue, scope, and deliverable

The official workshop currently lists 15 October 2026 for submission, up to 10 pages including references for full papers, IEEE two-column formatting, and an online workshop on 14 December. Recheck the official portal for deadline timezone, anonymity, and template instructions. Complete the paper by 13 October if possible.

Study a data-quality service receiving distributed IoT sensor streams for a digital-twin data space. GPU-based generative inference proposes and assesses alternatives; a graph database stores discovered associations and their evidence; a classic node-and-edge interface exposes disagreements to an operator. The implementation can analyze distributed data centrally. Do not label it federated learning or distributed training without implementing those capabilities.

A2. Research question, hypotheses, and novelty

Main question:
Can a generative model rank unreliable sensor observations and unreliable learned associations by testing proposed repairs on separate, unchanged witness evidence, and can the resulting findings carry useful calibrated confidence?

An observation is a measured value or interval. An association is a versioned, learned predictive relationship with endpoints, lag, applicable regime, and provenance. A disagreement is evidence that one or more current observations or associations are incompatible. A genuine physical-process anomaly can occur while every sensor reports correctly. These are distinct labels, not interchangeable names for the same event.

Research hypotheses:
H1: Separate witness evaluation improves fault localization over ordinary forecast/reconstruction error, especially when an anomalous source contaminates the predictions of other sensors.
H2: Testing observation edits and association masks separately improves identification of unreliable stored knowledge under relationship changes.
H3: Ensemble disagreement penalties and held-out calibration increase precision among accepted findings at a stated coverage level.
H4: Canonical provenance grouping prevents known duplicate messages from being treated as additional corroboration.

The intended improvement is targeted to nonlinear, regime-dependent, or conflicting evidence. A simpler method may be as good or better in linear stable data and should be expected to be cheaper. Include those favorable conditions for the alternatives in the experiments.

Compare against the full methods of GDN (AAAI 2021), CSDI (NeurIPS 2021), DiffAD (KDD 2023), TSAD-C, graph-guided counterfactual explanations by Shi et al. (BEHAIV 2025), Hara et al.'s graph anomaly localization (AISTATS 2015), generative counterfactual graph methods, and recent conformal time-series detection. Build a novelty matrix before expensive runs. Neither graph learning, diffusion, counterfactual perturbations, calibration, nor a database alone is novel. Assess the contribution as a specific scoring and evidence-validation method, supported by experiments.

A3. Exactly three primary datasets

A. Synthetic coupled sensor network
Create one documented simulator with several configurations, not several separately advertised datasets. Use latent physical states, heterogeneous sensor channels, lagged nonlinear coupling, exogenous operating regimes, correlated noise, and redundant measurements. Example model:
  z_t = A(r_t) z_(t-1) + B u_t + nonlinear(z_(t-1)) + process_noise
  x_i,t = h_i(z_t, z_(t-lag_i)) + bias_i(t) + measurement_noise_i,t
Use a physical process family independent of the learned diffusion architecture. Validate stability and dimensional units. Use 32 and 64 sensor nodes for primary experiments, with larger networks only for a measured scalability study.

Generate independent training, tuning, calibration, and test trajectories with different seeds and held-out operating regimes. Record latent truth, observation corruptions, association changes, source provenance, and affected intervals.

Fault families: offset, gradual drift, stuck-at, spike bursts, noise increase, dropout, replay/time delay, scale or unit mismatch, and small coordinated faulty groups. Relationship faults: wrong endpoints, wrong lag, wrong sign, unsupported associations, and stale associations after a genuine regime change. Match false and valid association candidates on simple properties so metadata does not reveal the answer. Include genuine consistent physical changes as controls and common-mode failures as difficult cases. Separate true process abnormalities from incorrect measurements.

B. Intel Berkeley Research Lab data
Official source: https://db.csail.mit.edu/labdata/labdata.html
Measured data from 54 wireless sensor motes with temperature, humidity, light, voltage, location, and communication information. Useful for spatially distributed sensing, missing observations, and redundant evidence.
Represent a physical mote separately from its channels. Preserve availability masks, original timestamps, and time since last observation. Choose resampling and any bounded interpolation using training data only. Do not interpolate across splits, long gaps, or known injected faults. Communication links are not automatically physical or causal dependencies.
There are no comprehensive authoritative sensor-fault labels established by the dataset description. Use a frozen, documented reference subset and injected faults for exact attribution, and describe this experiment as controlled corruption of real measurements. Native irregularities without adjudicated truth are qualitative cases, not scored proof of real faults. Quantify possible reference-data contamination.

C. SKAB
Official source: https://github.com/waico/SKAB
Measured physical water-circulation testbed signals including pressure, flow, motor current, voltage, vibration, and temperature. The repository provides anomaly/changepoint labels; freeze the exact version and verify file counts locally rather than assuming a planned future release exists.
Keep entire experiments disjoint across development, calibration, and test. Inspect documentation to determine what labels actually identify. Native labels support process-anomaly detection, not automatic identification of a broken sensor or false relationship. Add a separately reported controlled-corruption track on suitable held-out normal portions for precise observation/association attribution.
This is a compact physical testbed, not evidence of a large geographically distributed deployment. Intel and the simulator supply distributed-network coverage; SKAB supplies physical process validation.

Alternative only if required by data access or scientific fit: HAI, https://github.com/icsdataset/hai, is a larger industrial testbed augmented by hardware-in-the-loop simulation. Disclose its hybrid origin if substituted. SWaT is another strong option if authorized access is already available. Do not silently add a fourth dataset or wait for gated access before building the primary study.

A4. Established alternative methodologies and fair comparisons

Make PCA reconstruction-error detection the explicit simpler existing methodology. Use GDN as a stronger established graph comparator. Include a close diffusion detector to avoid a comparison that only demonstrates the value of a more expressive backbone.

Alternative 1: PCA reconstruction-error detection
Fit preprocessing and a rank-r PCA subspace on reference training data. For a standardized feature/window vector z_t in R^d and an orthonormal loading matrix P_r in R^(d x r):

  (B1) reconstructed_z_t = mean_z + P_r P_r^T (z_t - mean_z)
  (B2) Q_t = ||z_t - reconstructed_z_t||_2^2
  (B3) Q_i,t = mean over coordinates of sensor i of
                   (z_t - reconstructed_z_t)^2

Fit the PCA mean on training data even if earlier robust centering was performed. Choose rank using development reconstruction or labeled development metrics under the stated protocol. Include both contemporaneous PCA and a lag-embedded PCA variant so the baseline is not unnecessarily denied temporal information. Select a threshold on held-out calibration data and rank sensor contributions using Q_i,t. Do not use every principal component, which can make reconstruction error vanish.

Why it is expected to be less effective in some intended conditions: its standard subspace is linear; it does not sample multiple conditional explanations; reconstruction contributions do not uniquely distinguish a bad sensor from a stale relation. These are structural limitations and testable expectations, not already measured inferiority. Advantages: low computational cost, simple implementation, and potentially excellent performance on stable linear systems. A kernel-PCA extension is optional if basic nonlinear effects account for the entire observed advantage.

Alternative 2: Graph Deviation Network, GDN
Reproduce the published architecture/code, including learned sensor relationships and graph-based forecasting. In concise notation, its residual-style scoring is:

  (B4) predicted_x_t = graph_forecaster(x_(t-H):x_(t-1), learned_graph)
  (B5) residual_i,t = |x_i,t - predicted_x_i,t|
  (B6) standardized_residual_i,t =
          (residual_i,t - reference_median_i) / max(reference_IQR_i, epsilon)
  (B7) network_score_t = max_i standardized_residual_i,t

Follow the verified original details for the faithful reproduction. For the common protocol comparison, estimate all reference medians/scales from development reference data, use causal smoothing only, and calibrate every method without test-label optimization. Label this evaluation adaptation. Inspect the official evaluation script: it exposes test-optimal scoring paths and normalization behavior that must not be silently inherited into a leakage-free study. Preserve a faithful architecture while transparently harmonizing evaluation.

Expected difference: GDN provides forecast deviations and sensor localization; the proposed study explicitly tests observation and association hypotheses on separate witnesses. Do not claim GDN cannot localize faults. Its speed and simpler inference are advantages to report.

Required comparison ladder:
  B0: lag-embedded PCA residual detection.
  B1: GDN with the common calibration protocol.
  B2: the proposed diffusion backbone scored only by reconstruction/nonconformity.
  B3: the same repair method using deterministic conditional-mean replacements.
  B4: a close published diffusion anomaly detector, prioritizing TSAD-C or DiffAD.
  P: the full proposed score.

Do not assign zero association accuracy to a baseline that has no association output. Provide a separately labeled edge-residual extension using the same stored association predictors, or mark that task unsupported. Retain a simple direct association-prediction-residual baseline: a new method must improve on evaluating the relation's held-out prediction error directly. Compare equal data access, splits, candidate budgets, and calibration opportunities. Report both detection quality and compute cost.

A5. Model, notation, and equation-to-code specification

Notation:
X: a decision-time sensor window, represented as [batch, channel, time].
O: availability mask with the same shape; missing observations are not numeric zeros.
G: the versioned association graph available at the decision time.
h: a candidate observation interval or association to question.
E: independently trained model members, default 3 for the final method.
M: independent paired inference replicates per hypothesis, start at 8 or 16.
L: predictive samples per witness prediction, start at 8 or 16.
K_h: number of unique witness provenance groups available for h.
w_g: nonnegative group weight, sum_g w_g = 1; use uniform unique-group weights first.

Keep E, M, and L distinct. More stochastic samples from one model do not create more independently trained models. They reduce sampling noise but do not establish fault confidence. Each replicate m contains one generated repair and before/after predictive draws. Common random numbers may couple before/after noise to reduce variance, while replicate seeds remain independent.

E1. Training-only normalization
  s_i = max(IQR_train(x_i), epsilon_i)
  normalized_x_i,t = (x_i,t - median_train(x_i)) / s_i

Set epsilon_i from documented sensor resolution or a training-only scale floor, not test behavior. Treat constant channels explicitly. Save medians, scales, units, and excluded channels. CRPS may be computed in raw units and divided by s_i, or on normalized values with scale 1. Never apply both normalizations.

E2. Conditional diffusion training
Let A be a randomly selected target mask restricted to observed training cells, epsilon be Gaussian noise, and a_bar_s be the cumulative noise schedule:

  noisy_target_s = sqrt(a_bar_s) * target + sqrt(1-a_bar_s) * epsilon
  L_diff = E[ || A * (epsilon - denoiser_theta(noisy_target_s, s, context, G)) ||^2
                 / max(sum(A), 1) ]

Multiply and square elementwise before summing. The context must exclude the target values and their duplicate provenance copies. Known context is supplied separately rather than being mistaken for noised target data. Train with channel/time masking and edge dropout so inference-time masking is supported. This objective is an existing diffusion construction; the proposed scientific contribution is its use in the scoring procedure below.

Initial architecture: a compact temporal encoder with graph-conditioned cross-channel blocks and a conditional denoiser. Use PyTorch, mixed precision for training, and float32 or float64 accumulation for differences and final metrics. Start at windows of 64-128 steps and about 5-20 million parameters; adjust after profiling. Verify primary documentation before selecting current software versions.

E3. Association acceptance
Screen lagged dependencies on training data, then measure a candidate's predictive gain on separate development data:

  association_gain_e = mean_dev(loss_without_e - loss_with_e)

Use fixed targets, validation folds, a sparse neighbor cap, and block stability. Record which predictor defines this statistic and which graph attributes affect it. Store learned associations separately from known topology. Train-only discovery plus development validation precedes final score calibration.

If an experiment corrupts an edge's lag or sign, that attribute must affect the executable prediction/validation model. A decorative sign label ignored by the model cannot support a claim about detecting incorrect signs. A graph mask tests whether a relation is unhelpful; it does not prove the exact causal reason for its failure.

E4. Normalized empirical CRPS for one witness cell
For observed y, predictive samples v_1,...,v_L, and positive training scale s_i:

  C(v, y) = [ (1/L) sum_l |v_l-y|
               - (1/(2 L^2)) sum_l sum_k |v_l-v_k| ] / s_i

This is the CRPS of the empirical predictive distribution. Its finite-sample behavior depends on L; keep L equal across the comparisons where this score is used, and examine L sensitivity. For L=1 it reduces to normalized absolute error. The reference implementation uses the equivalent sorted-sample expression for the second term to avoid allocating an L-by-L pairwise tensor.

E5. Witness-group loss
Let J_g(h) be the fixed set of unique observed witness cells in group g, after masking unavailable targets and removing repair targets and duplicate descendants:

  loss_e,m,g(h) = [1 / |J_g(h)|] sum_(j,tau in J_g(h)) C(v_e,m,j,tau, x_j,tau)

The group losses for before and after must use identical J_g(h), counts, normalizers, prediction horizon, and weighting. Record a hash of the witness IDs. A group with no valid target cells is ineligible; an unavailable prediction causes an explicit failure/abstention, not a zero loss. Different hypotheses may have different support, so compare/calibrate them by type and support regime rather than assuming all raw scores are interchangeable.

Witness separation:
  - Generate observation repairs using proposal sources P_h.
  - Exclude witness target values W_h from the proposal context.
  - Exclude the suspect observation interval from proposal conditioning.
  - Predict each witness with its target interval and duplicate copies masked.
  - Evaluate predictions against actual unchanged witness observations.
  - Keep source partition rules fixed before examining test outcomes.

Other channels may still be statistically correlated. Distinct provenance IDs do not establish independence. Primary high-confidence attribution should require at least two distinct source groups when available; report low-support abstention and evaluate eligible single-group cases separately. Frozen screening and partition selection are part of the pipeline that is calibrated.

E6. Paired repair improvement
  Delta_e,m,g(h) = loss_before_e,m,g(h) - loss_after_e,m,g(h)

Observation hypothesis: replace only the stated cells using samples from the conditional generator. Association hypothesis: mask the specified association, retaining the same observed witnesses and their loss terms. Candidate masking must not change graph version history or measured records.

E7. Mean repair gain
  D_e,m(h) = sum_g w_g(h) Delta_e,m,g(h)
  R_e(h) = (1/M) sum_m D_e,m(h)
  R(h) = (1/E) sum_e R_e(h)

E8. Explicit model-instability penalty
  U(h) = sqrt( [1/(E-1)] sum_e (R_e(h) - R(h))^2 )

This rewrite makes U precise: it is the sample standard deviation of repair gains across separately trained models. Do not divide it by sqrt(E). It is a stability penalty, not a proven posterior standard deviation or formal confidence bound. Use evidence block bootstrap for metric intervals and sensitivity analysis separately rather than silently mixing several undefined uncertainties into U. If E=1, U is unavailable; label an uncertainty-free ablation instead of setting U=0 and reporting certainty.

E9. Edit cost
For an observation hypothesis with a fixed proposed edit mask H_h of k cells in a fixed local window containing N_h eligible cells:

  Omega_obs(h) = rho * k/N_h
                 + (1-rho) * mean_(e,m,(i,t) in H_h)
                    min( |replacement_e,m,i,t - x_i,t| / (c*s_i), 1 )

Use rho=0.5 and c=3 only as development starting values; freeze chosen values before final calibration. For association masking:

  Omega_edge(h) = number_of_masked_edges / max(number_of_edges_in_fixed_local_graph, 1)

The reference score accepts the already computed scalar cost. Production code must implement and audit the cost functions. The local graph and denominator are fixed before testing repairs. Costs for sensor edits and edge masks have different meanings: report/type-calibrate these outputs separately. Dropouts require explicit missingness hypotheses rather than pretending an absent observed value has a numeric edit magnitude.

E10. Proposed score to implement and investigate
  S(h) = R(h) - kappa * U(h) - lambda * Omega(h)

Use nonnegative kappa and lambda. Keep negative scores; do not clip at zero before ranking or calibration. Start kappa at 1 and lambda at 0.2 for arithmetic development only. Evaluate a small preregistered grid, e.g. kappa in {0, 0.5, 1, 2} and lambda in {0, 0.05, 0.2}, using development data. Calibrate the selected complete pipeline on untouched calibration data.

E11. Monte Carlo sampling diagnostic, distinct from E8
  SE_MC(h) = sqrt( [1/E^2] sum_e Var_m(D_e,m(h)) / M )

This estimates variability from the paired inference replicates conditional on the fitted ensemble, assuming replicate randomness is independent. Reusing one baseline forecast for all m omits baseline sampling variation; either regenerate/couple baseline draws per replicate or label the conditional approximation explicitly. Diagnose convergence as M and L increase. A zero SE_MC on constant toy arrays is not confidence that a fault is real.

E12. Null-tail calibration
For a fixed comparable score Z and B reference calibration units:
  p(Z) = [1 + sum_b I(Z_b >= Z)] / (B+1)

For candidate review, define the candidate type and selection-conditioned reference population. For a network/window alarm use Z_t = max_h S_t(h), running the same screening, support checks, and score pipeline on every reference calibration unit. Calibrating all maxima gives a window-level null-tail statement under the stated assumptions, not family-wise control over an unlimited sequence of windows. A window with no eligible candidate can receive a predeclared floor score below all finite scores; separately report insufficient evidence. Never quietly treat ineligible candidates as high-confidence negatives.

E13. Optional probability calibration
Fit an appropriate supervised calibrator on separate labeled calibration data, for example:
  q(h) = sigmoid(a * S(h) + b)

Fit/type-stratify where observation and association labels differ; use measured calibration quality, not the logistic shape alone, as justification. Document prevalence and unseen-fault transfer. The calibrated probability is conditional on the calibration population; 1-p from E12 is not q. If using the same dataset for null and probability calibration, use disjoint folds and avoid fitting both on the final test data.

E14. Decision semantics
Record three separate outputs: a process/anomaly alert, candidate attribution scores, and attribution acceptance/abstention. Use a calibrated conventional window nonconformity score for process alerts as well as the repair scores for attribution, since a genuine physical event need not be resolved by repairing a sensor. Do not suppress native process anomalies because no repair candidate passes. A lack of successful repairs does not prove the event is genuine; it may remain unresolved.

Output one or more plausible hypotheses if appropriate. If scores are close or evidence is inadequate, display competing possibilities. A top-two score margin may help a decision rule but is not a probability; tune it on development data. Multiple simultaneous sensor/edge faults can remain mixed rather than being forced into one exclusive class.

Numerical and integration checks:
  1. CRPS equals an independently written pairwise definition.
  2. A deterministic predictive distribution gives scaled absolute error.
  3. Consistent affine unit changes preserve normalized CRPS and edit cost.
  4. No-op edits yield zero gain; positive edit cost then lowers the score.
  5. Harmful edits have negative gain.
  6. Increasing lambda lowers scores by the specified cost difference.
  7. At fixed mean gain, larger model disagreement lowers S for kappa>0.
  8. Known duplicate identities do not change canonicalized inputs or scores.
  9. Conflicting copies with one ID are flagged, not silently averaged.
  10. Vectorized CPU and GPU formulas match an independent scalar calculation.
  11. Witness IDs, masks, horizons, and normalizers are identical before/after.
  12. Injecting a distinctive hidden witness value cannot affect repair generation.
  13. Labels, future samples, and repaired targets cannot enter their forbidden contexts.
  14. NaN/missing outputs cannot lower the loss through zero filling.
  15. p-values have the correct ordering, tie treatment, and resolution 1/(B+1).
  16. Deleting an edge does not delete a target or an error term.

A7. Limited theory and what the equations do not establish

Proposition 1, duplicate-provenance invariance:
With canonical deduplication before model input construction and again before group aggregation, unchanged unique evidence, fixed model parameters, and fixed inference randomness, adding exact known copies leaves losses, weights, R, U, costs, and S unchanged. Prove the invariance at each stage. If only the scoring kernel is deduplicated, claim only kernel invariance, since duplicated upstream messages can change model predictions.

Proposition 2, penalty sensitivity:
For fixed R, U, Omega, dS/dkappa = -U <= 0 and dS/dlambda = -Omega <= 0. This is a simple property of the defined score, not a deep new theorem. For two hypotheses, h1 outranks h2 exactly when:
  R(h1)-R(h2) > kappa[U(h1)-U(h2)] + lambda[Omega(h1)-Omega(h2)].
Use this inequality to explain observed ranking reversals in the sensitivity experiment.

Proposition 3, conditional calibration:
For a frozen complete scoring/selection pipeline and exchangeable calibration/test units under the declared null, the standard rank-tail value in E12 is super-uniform. Attribute this result to existing conformal theory. State whether the unit is a candidate, an experiment, or a window maximum. Temporal spacing alone does not establish exchangeability. Under serial dependence or distribution shift, show empirical false alarms and avoid an unproved exact guarantee.

Give an identifiability counterexample: two indistinguishable sensor explanations, or a common-mode fault shared by all informative witnesses, cannot be uniquely resolved from the observed relationships alone. Describe required informative redundancy, reliable enough conditioning evidence, and model adequacy. Do not claim a theorem of universal superiority over PCA/GDN. Keep the theory around half to one page, with detailed checks in reproducible artifacts if page limits require.

A8. Graph storage and exact visualization specification

Use Neo4j for versioned associations and evidence. Store raw arrays outside the graph with stable file/window references. Suggested entities: Sensor, Channel, ObservationWindow, AssociationVersion, Disagreement, RepairHypothesis, EvidenceGroup, ModelRun, and OperatorDecision. An association entity holds lag, sign/type where modeled, units, regime, discovery interval, validation gain, confidence status, provenance, and valid-from/valid-to values. It can be projected as an annotated edge in the operator's sensor graph.

Use Cytoscape.js or an equivalent graph library for the interactive view. This is a classic 2D node-link network, with conventional nodes connected by visible annotated edges. The paper must contain this actual style of diagram, not substitute an embedding plot, card layout, heatmap, or 3D graph. Those may support analysis elsewhere but do not satisfy the operator-diagram requirement.

Operator node-link encoding:
  - Circle: measured sensor/channel, with a short unambiguous identifier.
  - Optional small square: disagreement record, linked to the disputed items.
  - Solid directed edge: a stored predictive association; arrows mean prediction direction.
  - Thin gray: contextual association without a current flag.
  - Red double outline plus 'suspect reading': observation hypothesis.
  - Orange dashed edge plus 'association under review': relationship hypothesis.
  - Blue outline plus 'witness': evidence used in the current explanation.
  - Black dotted annotation connector: explanatory text, not another learned relation.
Do not rely on color alone or let edge thickness ambiguously mean both strength and uncertainty. Choose one meaning and explain it. Unknown confidence stays unknown.

Visible edge labels should name the relation and lag, e.g. 'predicts flow; lag 2 s'.
Disagreement annotation should show measured value, predicted interval, units, and status where space permits. Example wording without fabricated result numbers: 'Flow observation outside generated interval; sensor and edge hypotheses compared'. Full numbers come from the selected run.

Interface behavior:
Click a node/edge to show observed data, generated alternatives or prediction interval, R/U/Omega/S, calibrated p or probability with its proper meaning, unique witness sources, support count, candidate alternatives, graph/model version, and exact evidence links. Provide time selection, local neighborhood focus, and confirm/reject/defer controls. Decisions are logged; proposed repairs do not overwrite measurements or control physical equipment.

Use a local ego network with roughly 8-20 nodes for case figures. Keep all directly relevant witnesses and suspects; report any pruning rule. Fix coordinates across before/after panels. Intel sensor positions may guide layout; otherwise use a fixed seeded force-directed or layered layout, followed by reproducible position adjustment. Layout must not encode information from fault labels. Save graph JSON, positions, edge labels, styles, and run ID.

Produce SVG/PDF from exact graph data for the paper and a PNG preview for visual QA. Set figure fonts to remain readable, about 8-9 pt or larger at final placement. Inspect at the actual column/two-column width. Never use AI-generated imagery for the scientific graph or measured plots.

"""Numerical reference for the proposed score, not a trained fault detector.

Loss axes: [ensemble_member, paired_replicate, provenance_group].
The production PyTorch/CUDA implementation must agree with this reference.
"""
from __future__ import annotations

import json
import numpy as np


def normalized_empirical_crps(
    predictive_samples: np.ndarray,
    observed_values: np.ndarray,
    training_scale: np.ndarray | float,
) -> np.ndarray:
    """Score a finite predictive sample distribution; sample axis is last."""
    samples = np.asarray(predictive_samples, dtype=np.float64)
    observed = np.asarray(observed_values, dtype=np.float64)
    scale = np.asarray(training_scale, dtype=np.float64)
    if samples.ndim < 1 or samples.shape[-1] == 0:
        raise ValueError("At least one predictive sample is required.")
    if observed.shape != samples.shape[:-1]:
        raise ValueError("Observation shape must match all non-sample axes.")
    scale = np.broadcast_to(scale, observed.shape)
    if not all(np.isfinite(values).all() for values in (samples, observed, scale)):
        raise ValueError("Exclude missing targets explicitly; inputs must be finite.")
    if np.any(scale <= 0):
        raise ValueError("Use positive, frozen training scales.")
    sample_count = samples.shape[-1]
    first_term = np.mean(np.abs(samples - observed[..., None]), axis=-1)
    sorted_samples = np.sort(samples, axis=-1)
    ranks = np.arange(1, sample_count + 1, dtype=np.float64)
    coefficients = 2 * ranks - sample_count - 1
    second_term = np.sum(sorted_samples * coefficients, axis=-1) / sample_count**2
    result = (first_term - second_term) / scale
    if np.any(result < -1e-10):
        raise ArithmeticError("CRPS should not be materially negative.")
    return np.maximum(result, 0.0)


def canonicalize_groups(
    loss_before: np.ndarray,
    loss_after: np.ndarray,
    provenance_ids: list[str],
) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """Deduplicate exact repeated groups and return uniform unique-group weights.

    This is a final arithmetic guard. Real provenance must be deduplicated
    before model inputs and group losses are constructed as well.
    """
    before = np.asarray(loss_before, dtype=np.float64)
    after = np.asarray(loss_after, dtype=np.float64)
    if before.ndim != 3 or before.shape != after.shape:
        raise ValueError("Expected matching [ensemble, replicate, group] losses.")
    if before.shape[-1] != len(provenance_ids) or not provenance_ids:
        raise ValueError("Every group needs a canonical provenance identity.")
    if not np.isfinite(before).all() or not np.isfinite(after).all():
        raise ValueError("Losses must be finite.")
    first_indices: dict[str, int] = {}
    for position, identity in enumerate(provenance_ids):
        if not isinstance(identity, str) or not identity:
            raise ValueError("Provenance identities must be nonempty strings.")
        if identity in first_indices:
            previous = first_indices[identity]
            for values in (before, after):
                if not np.allclose(values[..., position], values[..., previous],
                                   rtol=1e-12, atol=1e-12):
                    raise ValueError("Conflicting copies require explicit provenance resolution.")
        else:
            first_indices[identity] = position
    selected = list(first_indices.values())
    weights = np.full(len(selected), 1.0 / len(selected), dtype=np.float64)
    return before[..., selected], after[..., selected], weights


def repair_score(
    loss_before: np.ndarray,
    loss_after: np.ndarray,
    group_weights: np.ndarray,
    edit_cost: float,
    uncertainty_weight: float = 1.0,
    edit_weight: float = 0.2,
) -> dict[str, float]:
    """Compute mean gain minus ensemble disagreement and edit penalties.

    Each replicate includes a repair and paired before/after predictive draws.
    Model standard deviation is NOT divided by sqrt(ensemble_count).
    Monte Carlo SE is a sampling diagnostic, not fault confidence.
    """
    before = np.asarray(loss_before, dtype=np.float64)
    after = np.asarray(loss_after, dtype=np.float64)
    weights = np.asarray(group_weights, dtype=np.float64)
    if before.ndim != 3 or before.shape != after.shape:
        raise ValueError("Expected matching [ensemble, replicate, group] losses.")
    ensemble_count, replicate_count, group_count = before.shape
    if ensemble_count < 2 or replicate_count < 2 or group_count < 1:
        raise ValueError("Need at least two models, two replicates, and one group.")
    if weights.shape != (group_count,):
        raise ValueError("One weight is required per unique provenance group.")
    if not all(np.isfinite(values).all() for values in (before, after, weights)):
        raise ValueError("Losses and weights must be finite.")
    if np.any(before < 0) or np.any(after < 0):
        raise ValueError("CRPS losses must be nonnegative.")
    if np.any(weights < 0) or not np.isclose(weights.sum(), 1.0):
        raise ValueError("Weights must be nonnegative and sum to one.")
    penalties = np.asarray([edit_cost, uncertainty_weight, edit_weight])
    if not np.isfinite(penalties).all() or np.any(penalties < 0):
        raise ValueError("Costs and penalty weights must be finite and nonnegative.")
    paired_gains = np.sum((before - after) * weights[None, None, :], axis=-1)
    model_gains = paired_gains.mean(axis=1)
    mean_gain = float(model_gains.mean())
    model_instability = float(model_gains.std(ddof=1))
    monte_carlo_variance = np.sum(paired_gains.var(axis=1, ddof=1) / replicate_count)
    monte_carlo_standard_error = float(np.sqrt(monte_carlo_variance / ensemble_count**2))
    score = mean_gain - uncertainty_weight * model_instability - edit_weight * edit_cost
    return {
        "score": float(score),
        "mean_gain": mean_gain,
        "model_instability": model_instability,
        "edit_cost": float(edit_cost),
        "monte_carlo_standard_error": monte_carlo_standard_error,
    }


def null_tail_value(calibration_scores: np.ndarray, candidate_score: float) -> float:
    scores = np.asarray(calibration_scores, dtype=np.float64)
    if scores.ndim != 1 or scores.size == 0 or not np.isfinite(scores).all():
        raise ValueError("Provide a nonempty finite one-dimensional calibration array.")
    if not np.isfinite(candidate_score):
        raise ValueError("The candidate score must be finite.")
    return float((1 + np.count_nonzero(scores >= candidate_score)) / (scores.size + 1))

        checks.append("conflicting_duplicate_evidence_is_rejected")
    else:
        raise AssertionError("Conflicting duplicated evidence was accepted.")

    noisy_before = generator.uniform(0.5, 1.5, size=(3, 9, 4))
    noisy_after = generator.uniform(0.1, 1.0, size=(3, 9, 4))
    nonuniform_weights = np.array([0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4])

    for model_index in range(3):

        for replicate_index in range(9):

                nonuniform_weights[group_index] *
